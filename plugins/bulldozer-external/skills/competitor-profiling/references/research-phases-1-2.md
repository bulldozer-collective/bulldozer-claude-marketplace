# Research Phases 1 and 2: Site Scraping and SEO Data

For each competitor, use `bdzWebExplorerSearch` + `bdzWebExplorerExtractContents` to pull and read the pages below (both are jeton-metered — don't fan them out to every page for a quick scan; extract only what the depth level below calls for):

| Page | Key Extractions |
|------|----------------|
| Homepage | Headline, subheadline, primary CTA, social-content proof claims, target audience |
| Pricing | Tiers, prices, feature breakdown, billing options, free tier/trial |
| Features | Core capabilities, how they describe each, differentiators |
| About | Team size, funding, founding story |
| Customers | Named logos, industries, case study themes |
| Changelog | Release velocity, recent product direction |

For team size, don't stop at whatever the About page claims — that's a number the company controls and rarely updates, usually a stale round figure. `bdzListLinkedInCompanies` (filter by `url`) returns the company's actual LinkedIn `employeeCount`, a real headcount instead of a vague "50+ employees" claim. It's also the field to track across a repeat profile of the same competitor: `employeeCount` keeps itself current once a `bdzCreateLinkedInRefreshConfig` (`targetType: COMPANY`) is set on it, while the OS's separate store of scraped LinkedIn profiles (`bdzListLinkedInEmployees` / its `totalElement` count) does not refresh on its own and measures something structurally different (stored profiles, not live headcount) — don't switch between the two counts across updates to the same profile, and don't read `totalElement` as a more precise version of `employeeCount`.

`bdzWebExplorerSearch`/`bdzWebExplorerExtractContents` read public pages — the same boundary applies as any other scraping. Fine against a homepage, a pricing page, or a public changelog; a different, weaker legal position the moment a page sits behind login, a paywall, or a robots.txt disallow (a competitor's customer portal, a gated whitepaper). Refuse to route around that — say what's unreachable in the profile rather than extract it anyway.

Add public ad activity where the company record exists in the OS: `bdzListPublicAds` (filter by `companyId`) returns actual ad creatives with an impressions range, platform, targeting (country/language/location) and start/end dates — a real read on current spend and messaging that a homepage scrape can't give you. Use it to corroborate or challenge the positioning claims from the site.

For review sites (deep profile only): Trustpilot via `bdzSearchReviews` (by domain — 404 means no Trustpilot profile) is the one review source Bulldozer OS genuinely fetches and stores for a competitor. Google Business Profile is not a real second option here, despite `bdzListReviews` accepting a `locationId` the same way it accepts a Trustpilot `companyId`: a GBP location only enters the OS through `bdzRegisterGbpLocation`, and the identifiers it needs come exclusively from `bdzSearchGbpLocations` — which lists, by its own description, "locations accessible via the project's connected GBP OAuth token." That's the client's own Google account, not an open lookup by business name — confirmed by schema, and by the internal Bulldozer project itself having zero registered GBP locations. There's no path from a competitor's public Google listing into that call unless the project happens to already hold OAuth access to the competitor's own Google account, which doesn't happen in a competitor-profiling context. Treat GBP the same as G2, Capterra and Product Hunt: no equivalent OS tool for a competitor, a one-off `bdzWebExplorerSearch` lookup of their public rating at most, never a routine data pull, and the number is a manual snapshot, not something the profile can refresh automatically. **Read a G2 or Capterra page as a solicited sample, not a spontaneous one** — a review-generation campaign (a vendor prompting happy customers, sometimes with an incentive) shapes who bothers to leave a rating, on a competitor's page as much as on the project's own. Carry it as a directional signal on positioning, never as proof of what a competitor's customers really think. **These review pools were never four independent readings to begin with.** Capterra, GetApp and Software Advice have shared a single review database under Gartner Digital Markets for years, predating G2's 2026 acquisition of the trio — only G2 itself was ever a separate pool. Since that acquisition closed (February 2026), the real structure is two pools under one owner, not four: G2 alone on one side, and the already-identical Capterra/GetApp/Software Advice trio on the other. The four platforms do share a unified buyer-intent signal since June 2026, but that's a separate, newer layer on top — the review pools themselves still aren't syndicated between the two sides (a review posted on G2 doesn't show up on Capterra, or vice versa, as of this writing). Treat a G2 rating and a Capterra (or GetApp, or Software Advice) rating for the same competitor as one campaign's result counted twice, not two corroborating populations — and don't expect a second opinion from comparing Capterra to GetApp or to Software Advice either, since those three were never different populations in any era. Extract overall rating, review count, top praise themes, top complaint themes, 3–5 representative quotes from whichever sources are actually reachable.

When Claap is connected to the project, real call transcripts are a stronger source for the same claim precisely because nobody asked the speaker to weigh in: `mcp__Claap__search_recording_transcripts` filtered by `tag: ["CompetitorMentions"]` (optionally `hasExternalSpeaker: true`) surfaces the moments a real prospect or customer named this competitor, each tied to its actual company and deal. Use it to corroborate or challenge a theme pulled from a review site before repeating it in the profile.

Bulldozer OS's own SEO tools replace a generic "DataForSEO or equivalent" pull:

- `bdzGetSeoDomains` (filtered by `domain`) — check whether the competitor's domain is already tracked. A 404 means it isn't.
- If tracked: `bdzGetSeoDomainOverview` (per market, e.g. `sources: ["FRANCE"]`) returns `rank`, `organicTraffic`, `brandedTrafficPercent`, `organicKeywordCount` and `organicCost` for the domain; `bdzGetSeoDomainKeywords` gives the ranked-keyword list; `bdzGetSeoBacklinksOverview` gives the backlink/referring-domains summary.
- If not tracked: `bdzCreateSeoDomain` registers it, but each declared market costs a provider call per monthly analysis (12 are seeded on creation) — don't register a competitor's domain by default for a quick scan; reserve it for a deep profile the user is committing to revisit, and declare only the markets that matter. Registering it doesn't mean it stays current on its own either — see the refresh-config check under Updating Profiles before assuming a number is fresh.
- **Competitor discovery** (who else ranks for the same keywords) has no dedicated OS tool — stays a manual read of the keyword list above, or a targeted `bdzWebExplorerSearch`.
