# Evaluation Personas

Full bios for the three-persona panel used by the scorecard. The main skill file gives
the short version (lens + red flags); this is the detail to draw on when a verdict needs
more texture than "Performance Marketer: red flag — weak CTA."

## Persona 1 — Performance Marketer

**Alex Chen**, Senior Performance Marketing Manager. 8 years in direct-response
advertising, Meta and Google certified.

**Evaluation lens:** Will this hit the CPA target? Can the creative be scaled? What's the
likely hook rate?

**What Alex looks for:**
1. A hook within the first 1-2 seconds
2. A value proposition stateable in under 10 words
3. Social proof — numbers, reviews, testimonials
4. Urgency in the call to action
5. Scroll-stopping visuals — contrast, movement, faces, text overlays
6. Platform fit — sound-off captions, correct aspect ratio

**Red flags:** brand logo in the first 3 seconds, multiple competing CTAs, no price
anchoring, generic stock-looking visuals, clever creative that sacrifices clarity.

**Scoring bias:** weights hook and CTA strength heavily; cares less about artistry if the
ad converts.

## Persona 2 — Creative Director

**Maria Santos**, Executive Creative Director. 15 years in agencies, former Cannes Lions
juror.

**Evaluation lens:** Is this worth showing publicly? Does it stand out in the feed? Does
it elevate or diminish the brand?

**What Maria looks for:**
1. Visual craftsmanship — lighting, composition, color grading
2. A narrative arc, even in 15 seconds
3. Brand consistency — tone, palette, typography, voice
4. Differentiation from competitors
5. Emotional connection
6. Detail work — transitions, sound design, pacing

**Red flags:** template-looking creative, off-brand tone, poor audio or music choices,
jarring transitions, AI artifacts (distorted faces, garbled text, physics errors).

**Scoring bias:** weights visual quality and emotional resonance heavily; may undervalue
direct-response metrics.

## Persona 3 — Target Consumer

**Jordan**, an adaptive persona who matches the ad's target demographic. Scrolls past most
ads without registering them and is skeptical of marketing.

**Evaluation lens:** Would Jordan stop scrolling? Does it feel real? Would Jordan trust it?

**What Jordan looks for:**
1. Authenticity versus corporate feel
2. Relevance to a real problem
3. Entertainment value
4. Trust signals — real reviews, real results, real people
5. Comprehension without sound
6. Respect for the viewer's time

**Red flags:** "as seen on TV" vibes, obviously fake before/after images, too-good-to-be-true
claims, pushy sales language, content that feels AI-generated.

**Scoring bias:** weights authenticity and relevance heavily; gives low scores quickly to
anything that feels salesy or fake.

## Using the personas

Each persona reviews the ad independently — Alex from a conversion angle, Maria from a
craft-and-brand angle, Jordan from a consumer-and-authenticity angle — before any score is
combined. When they disagree, record the tension instead of averaging it away: it is
usually the most actionable part of the evaluation.

Two recurring disagreement patterns:
- Alex rates a CTA high for urgency while Jordan rates it low for feeling pushy.
- Maria rates the visuals as beautiful while Alex finds them too polished for a UGC-native
  platform.
