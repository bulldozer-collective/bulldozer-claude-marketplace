# Red Account Playbook Details

## Red Account Playbook (continued)

**Before Day 0, sort the trigger — not every red is a relationship problem.** A payment-overdue flag is frequently a failed card or an invoicing snag, not dissatisfaction. Route it to billing/ops first to rule that out before anyone calls the customer. A CSM who opens with "I want to make sure we're delivering value" on an account that just had a billing hiccup reads as scripted, and manufactures doubt that wasn't there. Reserve the Day-0 executive call for triggers that are actually about the relationship — champion departure, usage collapse, unresolved escalations.

**Day 0:** CSM VP or designated executive contacts economic buyer by phone or direct email (not a form email). Subject: "I want to make sure we're delivering value." Tone: personal concern, not a save attempt.

**Day 2:** CSM pulls account health breakdown, identifies top 3 gap signals, and presents a 90-day recovery plan internally before presenting to the customer. If any gap signal is something the customer said directly — an exit-intent comment, a support ticket mentioning price or low usage — treat it as a symptom to verify, not the cause to plan against. A first stated reason ("too expensive," "not using it enough") often changes once someone asks a second question, and a recovery plan built on the first answer targets the wrong lever. Cross-check it against which dimension in the health score actually dropped before writing the 90-day plan.

**Day 5:** CSM or CS lead holds a working session with the account's champion (or identifies who the new champion should be if the original left). Outputs: documented success criteria revalidation, specific product actions agreed, next touchpoint in 7 days.

**Day 30:** Formal account check-in with executive sponsor from both sides. Deliver: quantified evidence of progress on recovery plan. Get: verbal commitment on renewal or documented risk if intent to churn.

**What not to do:** Don't offer discounts proactively — it signals the product isn't worth the price. Don't assign junior CSMs to red accounts. Don't run the generic QBR process with a red account — the red playbook replaces the QBR until the account is green.
