# Lead Lifecycle Stage Definitions and HubSpot Notes

## Stage Definitions

| Stage | Entry Criteria | Exit Criteria | Owner | SLA |
|-------|---------------|---------------|-------|-----|
| **Subscriber** | Opts in to content | Provides company info or shows engagement | Marketing | — |
| **Lead** | Identified contact with basic firmographic info | Meets minimum fit criteria | Marketing | — |
| **MQL** | Passes fit + engagement score threshold | Sales accepts or rejects | Marketing | Accept/reject within 24h |
| **SAL (Sales Accepted Lead)** | SDR has reviewed and accepted | SDR first contact made | SDR | First contact within 4h |
| **SQL** | Qualified via discovery conversation (BANT/MEDDIC) | Opportunity created or recycled | AE | Opportunity created within 48h |
| **Opportunity** | Budget, authority, need, timeline confirmed | Closed-won or closed-lost | AE | — |
| **Customer** | Closed-won | Expands, renews, or churns | CS | — |

**Stage hygiene rule**: No lead should sit in a stage without an SLA for more than the defined period. Implement automated alerts for violations.

**If the CRM is HubSpot**: the native `lifecyclestage` property ships with 8 values at install — Subscriber, Lead, Marketing Qualified Lead, Sales Qualified Lead, Opportunity, Customer, Evangelist, Other — not just the 5 people usually name. Two consequences for this table: **Customer already exists natively**, you don't need to create it as a custom stage; and **SAL is not one of the 8** — track it as a separate value on a "Lead Status" property (HubSpot's standard companion field for working a lead inside a lifecycle stage), not by adding a 9th value to `lifecyclestage`, which several downstream reports assume is one of the defaults. Whichever stages you keep, HubSpot does not move a record between them automatically — that logic (a workflow watching engagement/fit criteria and updating the property) has to be built; the field existing is not the same as the field being maintained.

**Don't assume "SAL" drops onto HubSpot's own Lead Status property either.** `hs_lead_status` is real and native, but its eight values are New, Open, In Progress, Open Deal, Unqualified, Attempted to Contact, Connected, Bad Timing — none of them says "sales-accepted." Either add a custom value to that property (one property, one source of truth) or track SAL on a separate custom property; don't write "Lead Status = SAL" into a spec as if the value already exists, because on a fresh portal it doesn't.

**Before building that custom value, check whether the portal has HubSpot's native LEAD object (Sales Hub) turned on — it's a better fit for SAL than a Contact property.** LEAD is a first-class object, separate from Contact and `lifecyclestage`: "Tracks progress of associated contact or company through sales qualification. Leads are eventually either qualified or closed." Each Lead has its own pipeline, and unlike every other pipelined object (`state`: OPEN/CLOSED), a Lead's stages carry one of four fixed states — NEW, IN_PROGRESS, QUALIFIED, UNQUALIFIED — which maps onto SAL far more cleanly than a value bolted onto `hs_lead_status`: a lead entering IN_PROGRESS already *is* sales-accepted, no custom value to invent or maintain. Two things to check before recommending it over the Lead Status workaround: whether the client's HubSpot connection has actually granted `crm.objects.leads.read` (on one portal checked, `discover_hubspot_schema` found the object fine but a direct property read failed on exactly that missing scope — a reauthorization step, not a tier limit) and whether the pipeline stages configured under each fixed state actually mean what their labels suggest (a real portal's "IN_PROGRESS" stages were five retry-cadence steps, not a generic qualification stage — read the client's actual pipeline before writing stage names into the spec). The two mechanisms — `lifecyclestage` and LEAD — coexist and don't replace each other automatically; decide which one the client is actually driving before writing SAL tracking into either.

**"Budget, authority, need, timeline confirmed" is not proof unless "authority" names the economic buyer, not just an engaged champion.** The classic way an Opportunity dies late is a rep with a warm, responsive contact who talks like they can decide — and can't: a champion can move the process internally, but only the economic buyer can kill it with one no. Before an Opportunity is created, the discovery conversation should produce a name (or a credible path to one) for whoever can actually say yes to the money — not just enthusiasm from whoever picked up the phone.

**A cheap first filter, if the CRM is HubSpot: `num_associated_contacts` on the deal.** It's a native, auto-filled counter — no report to build. A deal sitting at `num_associated_contacts = 1` after months in pipeline is a single-threading flag worth raising before anyone re-reads call notes to check who's actually been in the room. It doesn't replace the champion/economic-buyer check above — HubSpot does have a native "Buying Role" field on the contact (`hs_buying_role`: Blocker, Budget Holder, Champion, Decision Maker, End User, Executive Sponsor, Influencer, Legal & Compliance, Other; multi-select, a contact can carry several roles at once) — but it lives on the **contact**, not per deal. A contact who is Champion on one open deal and just an End User on another can't hold two different roles there; for that, tag the role on the Contact-to-Deal association itself (an association label such as "Champion" scoped to this one deal-contact pair) or, for a fuller picture of the whole buying committee, use the Buying Groups tool (Sales Hub). Either way it's a real signal to read, not a field to build from scratch — just don't stop at the contact property alone once the same buyer shows up on more than one deal.
