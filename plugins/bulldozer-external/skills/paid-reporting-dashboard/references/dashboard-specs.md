# Dashboard Specifications

**Before building any of these four by hand, check whether HubSpot already has a saved report covering the same slice** (`manage_saved_reports`, action `LIST`) — reusing an existing report is faster than rebuilding it from a CRM query. On at least one portal this check itself comes back blocked with a permissions message that, unlike every other HubSpot scope block documented in this stack, never names the missing scope — just a generic "you need access to additional permissions... work with your HubSpot administrator" reply. Treat that as its own seventh failure mode, not a token to refresh: tell the client's admin a HubSpot permissions review is needed, without inventing a plausible-looking scope name to hand them.

### Dashboard 1: Executive (Weekly, CEO / Board)

Purpose: Paid investment → pipeline → revenue

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Pipeline from paid (30-day) | Sum of deal values created where Deal Source = paid, current month | Match % of revenue target from paid channel |
| Pipeline from paid (90-day) | Same filter, last 90 days | 5–10x ad spend |
| 180-day ROAS | Closed-won ARR (Deal Source = paid, last 180 days) ÷ ad spend | 4.5–8.5x for B2B SaaS |
| Cost per SQL | Total ad spend ÷ SQLs with paid source | €350–750 mid-market |
| CAC payback period | CAC ÷ (MRR × gross margin %) | 14–18 months (Series B median — see note below) |

**Why 180-day ROAS:** B2B sales cycles vary a lot by deal size and have gotten longer in recent years — aggregated 2026 benchmarks put the median SaaS cycle in the 80–95 day range overall, with mid-market running 60–120 days and enterprise 90–180+, so no single number applies to every company. Two named vendor reports converge on the same order of magnitude without fully agreeing — Gartner is cited (secondhand, no report title found) at an 84-day B2B SaaS average, and RAIN Group's 2025 B2B Sales Cycle Benchmark puts the mid-market median at 92 days, up from 68 in 2019 — while others diverge sharply (one 2026 industry report puts SaaS specifically at 67 days, another puts enterprise as high as 540). Treat the 80–95 range as directional convergence, not a settled figure: no primary report was read directly for this skill (secondary-source summaries only). Evaluating paid ROI at 30 days systematically understates performance whatever your actual cycle length is — most deals from this month's ads haven't closed yet. 180 days is a safe minimum window regardless; check your own CRM's average days-to-close, and extend the window if it runs past 150 days.

**Which targets above are sourced, and which aren't.** CAC payback is the only row with a citable number behind it: the 2026 Aleph × Benchmarkit SaaS & AI Performance Benchmarks (342 companies, FY2025 actuals) puts the median at 16 months, with Series B companies at 14–18 and a range of roughly 9–24 months across deal-size tiers — the table above uses the Series B figure as this skill's default context; recalibrate to your own stage and ACV. A second, larger vendor sample published later in 2026 (Optifai, ~939 B2B SaaS companies) lands in the same range without contradicting it — 11 months below €5K ACV widening to 22 months at €50–100K ACV — one more point of convergence, not a reason to move the band above. The other three targets (90-day pipeline multiple, 180-day ROAS, cost per SQL) have no equivalent analyst-grade source behind them — every public "B2B SaaS ROAS benchmark" found is an unaudited agency blog, and the numbers across them disagree by 2–4x for the same metric. Treat those three as a placeholder until you have 2–3 quarters of your own trailing data, then replace them — a scorecard measured against someone else's average isn't a scorecard. And when summing deal values for this table, aggregate on the CRM's currency-converted amount field, not the raw one, if deals span more than one currency.

### Dashboard 2: Pipeline Attribution (Weekly, Marketing + RevOps)

- Pipeline by paid channel (Google / LinkedIn / Meta / other) — current quarter vs. last quarter
- MQL → SQL conversion rate by original source — which channels produce leads that close?
- Lead volume by campaign × lifecycle stage — are campaigns producing pipeline or just form fills?
- Cost per SQL by campaign — which specific campaigns are worth scaling?

### Dashboard 3: Campaign Performance (Daily, Paid Media)

- Spend, impressions, clicks, CTR by campaign
- Form fills and cost per form fill by campaign
- Offline conversions (MQL, SQL) by campaign — the leading indicator of pipeline quality
- Audience overlap and frequency cap monitoring
- **Already in the OS:** once accounts are registered, `bdzGetAdAnalytics` returns spend, impressions, clicks, CTR, CPC, CPL, conversions and cost-per-conversion at three levels — campaign, ad group/ad set, or individual ad/creative (`target` = `AD_ANALYTICS_TARGET_LAYER_1` / `_LAYER_2` / `_LAYER_3`, one specific entity via `adLayerId`, or the matching `_ALL` variant for every entity on the account) — for Meta, LinkedIn, Google and Microsoft Ads. This dashboard can pull from there instead of a manual platform export. The ad group/ad set level (`_LAYER_2`) is the one most teams skip straight past, jumping from campaign to creative — it's the right level for "which audience or placement is dragging this campaign down" before touching bids or creative, and the level to reach for when a client specifically asks for a breakdown by ad group rather than by campaign. Pull it with an explicit `from`/`to` matching this dashboard's own window (a day or a few days) — left blank, the tool defaults to 180 days back, and a "daily" dashboard built on that default quietly turns into a six-month rolling average, hiding the overnight break this dashboard exists to catch.
- **For a period-over-period number anywhere in this stack — this dashboard's day-over-day, or Dashboard 4's quarter-over-quarter channel trend — use `bdzCompareAdAnalytics` instead of two separate `bdzGetAdAnalytics` calls.** Same `target`/`adLayerId` rules as above, but unlike `bdzGetAdAnalytics` none of its four bounds default: `currentFrom`, `currentTo`, `previousFrom` and `previousTo` are all required, so there's no silent 180-day fallback to worry about here. Rows from both periods come back already paired by entity, with `absoluteDelta` and `percentageDelta` computed server-side per metric — read those directly, never recompute a delta from the raw `current`/`previous` objects, and never re-derive a ratio (CTR, CPC, CPL, ROAS): each period's ratio is already derived from that period's own sums. A `current` or `previous` that comes back `null` means the entity is new or stopped in that period, not a fetch error. **Check `freshness` before reading any delta as a real change** — more so here than on a single pull: an incomplete current period (an account with `upToDate: false`) produces a negative delta indistinguishable from a genuine drop unless you check first.
- **Check `freshness` before reading any number as "the overnight break."** The tool's numbers are only as current as the last successful import, not live — read the `freshness` entry for the ad account first. When `upToDate` is `false`, the totals stop at that entry's `dataThrough` and the rest of the requested window is *missing*, not zero: a flat or dropping spend line that's actually a stalled import (`lastAttemptErrorKind` names why — a token gone missing or expired is the common one) looks identical to a real overnight break on this dashboard unless you check it first. `bdzListAdImports` on the same account gives the fuller failure history if `lastAttemptState` alone isn't enough to act on. **`upToDate: false` has two different causes, and only one is yours to chase:** confirmed on a live project, the same `freshness` list can carry one account with a named `lastAttemptErrorKind` (a real stalled import — go fix it) sitting right next to another with `refreshEnabled: false` and no error at all — an account someone deliberately pulled out of the daily refresh (commonly one paused or dormant, kept registered for its history). That second shape is stale by design, not broken; chasing it as a tracking break burns the five minutes this check is supposed to save.
- **A LinkedIn Sponsored Messaging (Message Ad) campaign reports through a different set of fields on this same call — not a stripped-down version of the usual ones, a different contract entirely.** Confirmed in the tool's own schema: since LinkedIn's July 2024 change this format returns `null` for `clicks`/`ctr` (true for every platform and campaign type, not a gap specific to Message Ads), and its `impressions` is literally the same count as `sends` — not a tracking break. Read `sends`, `opens`, `openRate`, `buttonClicks` (the message's action button), `bannerClicks` (the banner unit beside it), `linkClicks` (links in the body), `costPerSend` and `costPerOpen` for this format instead of the spend/impressions/clicks/CTR row this dashboard otherwise pulls. A Message Ad row showing zero clicks here is the format working as designed — check the campaign's objective/type before logging it as a broken pixel in the Data integrity step of the Bi-Weekly Review.
- **Two more fields ride along on the same call, worth knowing before reaching for them on this dashboard: `revenue` and `roas` (revenue ÷ cost).** Confirmed by a real call against a live project: they come back `null` on most rows (the platform recorded no monetary value for that conversion), but at least one row came back a real `0.0` instead — a different statement, meaning the platform *did* evaluate a value-bearing event and it priced at zero, not that none fired. In practice only Meta (a purchase or value-bearing custom conversion) and, now, LinkedIn (its own conversion value) can ever fill these through this tool — Google Ads and Microsoft Ads never do here; their own ROAS lives in the SEA-specific metrics tools instead. **Never read this `roas` as a substitute for the dashboard's 180-day ROAS, or feed it into Dashboard 1 — that's exactly the blend the first Rule below exists to prevent:** it's the platform's own attribution of value it was told about, not Deal Source revenue from the CRM. Where it earns its place is a same-day sanity check on *this* dashboard, for a campaign where you've already assigned it a real conversion value (Layer 3) — never as the input to a reallocation decision.

### Dashboard 4: Channel Mix (Monthly, CMO)

- Budget allocation vs. pipeline attribution by channel — are we spending in proportion to what produces revenue?
- Win rate by original source — which channels produce buyers vs. browsers?
- CAC by channel — what does it cost to acquire a customer from each channel?
- Quarter-over-quarter trend by channel — on the ad-spend side of this trend, pull it with `bdzCompareAdAnalytics` rather than two separate calls (see Dashboard 3 above for the contract)
- **Before summing spend across channels:** `bdzListRegisteredAdAccounts` lists every connected account with its currency — check it first. Currency can come back empty on an account that hasn't refreshed yet, and two accounts in the same project aren't guaranteed to share one currency; a CAC-by-channel number that blends them without checking is wrong in a way that won't show up until someone compares it to the CRM.
