# Commission Structure Notes: New vs. Existing Business, Multi-Year Deals, Caps

If HubSpot is the connected CRM, its native `dealtype` field gates a differentiated rate without building a custom property — out of the box it carries two values, New Business (`newbusiness`) and Existing Business (`existingbusiness`), and HubSpot's generic field description ("categorize your deal as either a New Business or Existing Business") reads as if that's a closed list. It isn't a guarantee: `dealtype` is a standard picklist, and a portal admin can add custom options (`renewal`, `upsell`) without that description ever changing. Don't take the generic description as proof the field is still closed to two values on a given client's portal — call `get_properties` on `dealtype` for *that* portal first. If it comes back with more than the two defaults, use the finer split directly instead of building a redundant custom property; if it's still just the two, a renewal, an upsell, and a cross-sell all land in Existing Business with no further split, and a plan that wants a different rate for pure renewal versus expansion needs either a custom property or a read on the deal's associated line items — `dealtype` alone doesn't get you there.

A meaningfully lower rate on Existing Business than on New Business is common practice — new-logo acquisition is repeatedly described as the higher-paying motion in 2026 sales-comp content, renewal the lower-paying one, with expansion/upsell landing somewhere between — but treat this the same as every other rate cited in this skill: the specific percentages circulating are vendor-blog convergence, not a named, audited study, so pick the split from what the deal actually costs to originate, not a number copied from a benchmark page. Apply the same "less origination work, lower rate" logic used below for team-selling and partner-sourced deals — the plan is only internally consistent if that principle points to the same kind of discount everywhere it applies, not a different one each time.

### Multi-Year and Backloaded Deals

Pay full commission on Year 1 ACV only, never on the full total contract value of a multi-year deal. A 3-year contract signed at once shouldn't pay 3× the commission of an equivalent 1-year deal closed three times — the rep did one sale, not three. A separate, lower rate on Year 2/3 revenue (recognized as it renews) rewards the rep for structuring a longer commitment without creating a windfall on revenue the company hasn't collected yet, and keeps the incentive aligned with a normal renewal rate elsewhere in the plan. This pattern shows up repeatedly across 2026 SaaS comp-design content, but — same reservation as the ratios above — no single audited source backs a specific split; treat the two-tier structure (full Year 1, reduced multi-year) as the shape to copy, not any particular percentage as a verified number.

**Don't answer the "what if a rep closes a $2M fluke deal" question with a hard cap**: a maximum dollar amount or percentage beyond which no further commission is paid punishes the exact overperformance the accelerator table above is built to reward — and a rep who gets capped once remembers it for years. 2026 comp-design content converges on two narrower tools instead, both aimed at the single outlier rather than at ordinary strong performance: a **windfall clause** (a reduced rate on the portion of any one deal above a stated size, so one freak deal doesn't blow the plan's budget) and a **whale-deal clause** (any single deal above a threshold — commonly framed as roughly half a rep's annual quota — triggers a finance review before commission is paid, which guards against gaming without touching a normal big win). Set the threshold from the client's own deal-size history, not a copied number: a threshold that catches an ordinary enterprise deal for this business is a cap wearing a different name.
