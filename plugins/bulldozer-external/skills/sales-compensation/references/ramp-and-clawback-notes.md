# Ramp and Clawback Notes

**The ramp itself is lengthening industry-wide**: The Bridge Group's "AE Models, Motions & Metrics: 2026 Research Report" — its 10th biennial study of the AE role, ~158 B2B companies surveyed — is the first named, dated study found on this specific question; earlier checks on this skill had found none. As of early October 2026, an aggregator citing the report directly attributes a blended AE ramp of 6.2 months across all segments — described as the highest the study has recorded — and, for the first time, ties a 9–12 month enterprise figure to this same report rather than to an unattributed blog estimate. The report itself still isn't readable directly in this environment (its own domain stays unreachable here), so this remains an aggregator's attribution, not a confirmed quote — treat it the same way as every other number in this skill: directional, not a reason to rewrite the 6–9 month enterprise range above on an unconfirmed attribution alone, but a sign that range may need to move toward 9–12 months once the study itself can be read.

**First hire in the role**: this table assumes a team the new rep can be ramped against — an existing quota history, a senior peer to shadow, a manager who has run this ramp before. For a company's first AE or first SDR, none of that exists yet. Don't copy the percentages above as a target; set the ramp milestones as an input from the founder or the best generalist closer in the company (whoever currently does the closing), and treat the first two quarters as the source of the quota history this table will be built on next time — not as a plan the rep is expected to hit.

**Anchor the window to a stated date, not "whenever it churns"**: write the 90–120 days as counted from contract start (or from the deal's `closedate` in HubSpot), not from an ambiguous "close" that different people in the room read differently. A clawback clause that doesn't name its own start date is unenforceable the first time a rep disputes it — the same discipline as writing the deal-split rule down before the first disputed deal, above.

**Where clawback needs a reason code, not an automatic trigger**: a churn inside the window caused by a genuine mis-sell (a fit the rep should have caught, a promise that wasn't kept) should be clawed back without debate. A churn caused by a failed onboarding, a missed handoff, or a support failure is a Customer Success outcome, not a sales one — clawing back the rep's commission for it teaches reps to distrust the CS handoff, not to qualify harder. Require the reason to be recorded before the clawback fires, not applied automatically off the churn date alone.

**Have the clause reviewed against local wage law before shipping it**: commission clawback enforceability depends on wage-payment rules that vary by state/country and change independently of sales-comp practice — this skill can design the mechanism, but the signed plan document should get a compliance pass from whoever handles employment law for the client, not skip straight from draft to rollout.
