# Building the Content — Sources Ranked by Reliability

1. **Win/loss interviews** — The only source that tells you what actually happened in the room. Buyers who evaluated both products tell you what they noticed that reps never see. Feed win/loss findings directly into the "when we win / when we lose" sections. When Claap is connected to the project, this is not purely manual: real call transcripts tagged `CompetitorMentions` and `Objections` surface the moments a named competitor actually came up — see *Sourcing and Refresh in Bulldozer OS* below.

2. **Trustpilot reviews (native), G2 / Capterra / GetApp / Software Advice reviews (manual)** — Real user language for weaknesses. Look for recurring themes in the middle-rating band (too positive to be fake, honest enough to surface real gaps). Filter for reviews from your target segment. Trustpilot is a native Bulldozer OS source; the other four are not — they mean a manual web search, not a refreshable feed. See *Sourcing and Refresh in Bulldozer OS*. **Read any of these pages as a solicited sample, not a spontaneous one** — a review-generation campaign (a vendor prompting happy customers, sometimes with an incentive) shapes who bothers to leave a rating, on your competitors' pages as much as your own. Treat it as a directional read on positioning, useful for spotting a recurring weakness worth checking elsewhere — never as proof of what a competitor's customers really think. The unsolicited call excerpts in source 1 (`CompetitorMentions`, `Objections`) carry more weight for the same claim precisely because nobody asked the speaker to weigh in. **These four sites are not four independent audiences — they're two.** Capterra, GetApp, and Software Advice have shared a single Gartner Digital Markets review database for years, long before any 2026 ownership change: a rating that matches across those three never confirmed two separate populations, at any point in time. G2 is the one genuinely separate pool, now under the same owner as the other three (deal closed February 2026, vendor-submission flow unified since June 2026). So a match between G2 and any one of Capterra/GetApp/Software Advice confirms the reach of one solicitation campaign, not independent corroboration — but a match among Capterra, GetApp, and Software Advice was never a second data point to begin with. Don't count either kind of match as independent confirmation of a weakness — corroborate it against a source with a genuinely different audience instead (Trustpilot, or the unsolicited Claap excerpts above).

3. **Job postings** — Competitors' job postings reveal their tech stack, priorities, and organizational gaps. A competitor posting 5 customer success roles signals churn problems. A competitor posting implementation engineers signals slow deployment.

4. **Pricing pages** — What they publish, what they hide. Hidden pricing = enterprise-only = long sales cycle. Per-user pricing = seat-count sensitivity = landmine if your prospect has a large team.

5. **Their sales team's outbound** — If your reps are being contacted by competitor SDRs, forward the messages. How they position against you is how they're being coached to position against you.

6. **Your own sales calls** — Pattern the objections. If "Competitor X's integrations" comes up in 5 calls this quarter, that's a battlecard update trigger.
