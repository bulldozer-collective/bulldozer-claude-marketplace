# Sourcing and Refresh in Bulldozer OS — Detail

**Weaknesses, from reviews.** `bdzSearchReviews` imports Trustpilot for a bare domain (`acme.com`, not the full URL) and stores them; `bdzListReviews` reads them back with `minRating` / `maxRating`, which is exactly the middle band the sources section asks you to mine. Four things to know: an import is capped at 200 reviews, a second import inside a week returns what's stored rather than re-fetching, a 404 means Trustpilot has no profile for that domain, and a 502 means Trustpilot itself couldn't be reached that time — retry once before concluding there's nothing to import.

**What they're claiming right now.** `bdzListPublicAds` returns a competitor's public ads for a date range. Their live ad copy is their current positioning, dated — more reliable than a homepage that may have been written two years ago, and the fastest way to notice a repositioning between quarterly reviews.

**Strengths, sourced instead of asserted.** `bdzGetProjectCompetitionIntensity` scores every tracked competitor on 7 channels (SEO, SEA, CRO, GEO, Meta, LinkedIn, social) and returns 3 sourced strengths per competitor plus an estimated monthly media budget — this is real material for "THEIR GENUINE STRENGTHS," not a guess at what they're good at. Two constraints: it's a project-wide async analysis (state RUNNING/SUCCESS/ERROR, judge freshness on `startedAt`), and regenerating it always recomputes every tracked competitor at once — never something you trigger "for this one card." Read the existing analysis first; only propose regenerating it when it's stale or missing. Both this call and the one below require a `targetProjectId` on top of the usual `customerId`/`projectId` — pass the project's own id again there, or the call is rejected.

**Sizing them, directionally.** `bdzGetProjectOfferStructure` doesn't return a competitor's real revenue or headcount — it returns a market-type verdict (e.g. fragmented) and, per tracked competitor, an estimated revenue and headcount pulled from partial public sources (Crunchbase, LinkedIn, Societe.fr), with the tool's own note that these can diverge significantly from reality. Useful for a rough sense of scale behind the OVERVIEW line ("sells to who, at what price range") — never as a number to defend if a prospect pushes back on it.

**Pricing pages, job postings, funding.** `bdzWebExplorerSearch` finds the pages (`includeDomains` to stay on their site, `startPublishedDate` to see only what appeared since the last review) and `bdzWebExplorerExtractContents` reads them. Both are metered: one pass per review, not a crawl. Bulldozer OS also has a native way to turn job-posting monitoring into an ongoing feed instead of a one-off search: the Signal system (`SIGNAL_TYPE_JOB_OFFERS_WATCHLIST`, scoped to a named list of companies) surfaces new postings for tracked competitors automatically. It's not the default here — a Signal requires its own lead list and bills jetons per lead it produces, more setup than a single card's quarterly refresh needs. Reach for it only when the volume of postings to watch justifies standing infrastructure rather than a manual pass; see *competitive-hiring* for that fuller method, including the monthly headcount-velocity calculation below.

**Headcount, as a hard number.** For a competitor whose LinkedIn company page is already known, `bdzCreateLinkedInRefreshConfig` (`targetType: COMPANY`, monthly is enough for a card) keeps `bdzListLinkedInEmployees` current — it returns a stored, exact employee count (`totalElement`) rather than a follower count or an estimate. That's a firmer number than the offer-structure sizing below for the OVERVIEW line's scale, and a useful cross-check on "posting 5 customer success roles" style claims in THEIR REAL WEAKNESSES. A single snapshot is enough for a card; tracking the month-over-month change is *competitive-hiring*'s job, not this skill's.

**Where the evidence lives.** `bdzSetCompetitorNote` holds the sourced evidence and its date behind each claim on the card; `bdzGetCompetitorNote` reads it back. The card is the field artifact and stays one page — the note is where the next owner finds out why a line says what it says, without redoing the research.

**Between reviews.** `bdzGetCompetitorRefreshConfig` shows what refreshes automatically for that competitor, `bdzUpdateCompetitorRefreshConfig` sets it — all four flags (`refreshAds`, `refreshReviews`, `refreshSeo`, `refreshSea`) are required on write, so read the current config first. Turning on ads and reviews for every carded competitor is what makes the update triggers fire on their own instead of waiting for the quarter.

**What the OS does not give you.** Reviews are Trustpilot and Google Business Profile — there is no G2, Capterra, GetApp, or Software Advice feed, so a rating shift on any of those stays a manual check, not something Bulldozer OS can watch for you. And since all four sites now sit under one owner (Capterra, GetApp, and Software Advice already shared one review database long before that — see source 2 above), that manual check covers at most two independent properties, not four: read a shift on any of Capterra, GetApp, or Software Advice as the same signal, and a shift on G2 as the other one. Full pipeline-wide loss patterns (which deals a competitor appeared in, across the whole quarter) are CRM/CS work beyond a single card: see *win-loss-analysis*. And "when we lose" still needs a human read of the evidence — the transcripts and closed-won deals above are raw material, not a pre-written sentence.
