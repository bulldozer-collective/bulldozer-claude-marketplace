# Two Judgment Calls a Roadmap Has to Make

**The signaling hire.** A board member or an investor pushes for a VP Sales hire "to show we're serious about scaling" — before 2 AEs are hitting quota, before the motion is proven. This is the single most common way the override table gets bent for the wrong reason: not one of the five real conditions (enterprise ACV, AI tooling, PLG, burn constraint, new market), just optics. Refuse it as a full-time VP hire. If the board genuinely needs a senior operator in the room now, the honest answer is a fractional or interim sales leader — someone paid to diagnose and coach for a fixed period, not someone whose job is to scale a motion that doesn't exist yet. Naming the real motive (signaling, not bottleneck) is more useful to the client than quietly complying with it.

**When to stop the sequence instead of continuing it.** A roadmap is a bet that the diagnosed bottleneck is the real one. Set the threshold before the hire, not after: if a hire has been fully ramped (per the Step 6 ramp times) for one full quarter and the bottleneck metric from Step 1 hasn't moved — pipeline coverage still short after the SDR ramped, win rate still flat after the enablement hire ramped — that's a signal the original diagnosis was wrong, not that the hire needs more time. Re-run Step 1 before adding the next role on the calendar. Proceeding to hire #2 on a sequence whose hire #1 didn't move the number it was meant to move is sunk-cost hiring: the plan looks disciplined because it's being followed, not because it's working.
