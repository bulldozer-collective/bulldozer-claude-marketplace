# LTV Expansion Levers

**Lever 1: Subscription / Subscribe & Save**
- Best for: Beauty, supplements, coffee, food — anything replenishable
- Converts 5–15% of customers into recurring revenue
- Tool: ReCharge or Bold Subscriptions — check current positioning before committing: the subscription-app market is consolidating (Recharge acquired rival Skio for $105M in April 2026; both still run as separate products today), a single-app recommendation is a snapshot, not a permanent answer
- Churn: model against your own category, not a flat number — 2026 aggregate benchmarks run 6.5–8.5% monthly overall (below 7% is solid, below 5% is top-quartile), but the range is wide by category: coffee and consumables sit closer to 5–8%, meal kits and beauty boxes run 8–15%. Model this before building, since high subscription churn can hurt more than it helps.
- Split voluntary from involuntary churn before diagnosing it — they're two different problems. Voluntary (customer cancels) is usually 60–75% of the total and gets fixed with win-back flows, pause/skip flexibility, and the LTV levers elsewhere in this step. Involuntary (payment failed and retries ran out) is the other 25–40% and doesn't respond to any of that — it needs a card-updater/dunning stack (retry logic, updated-card capture) on the payment side. A program that only builds win-back emails is leaving a third or more of its churn unaddressed by construction.
- Lock in a subscriber at 15% discount, and even with 10% monthly churn, you're typically ahead by month 3 — a rule-of-thumb payback calculation to sanity-check against your own margin, not a cited benchmark.

**Lever 2: Bundling and Upsell**
- Cart upsell: Display a complementary product at checkout (Shopify native or Zipify OCU)
- Post-purchase upsell: Offer a bundle or complementary product on the order-confirmation page via Shopify's Post-Purchase Checkout Extensions (the current name for this capability under Checkout Extensibility) or an app such as Zipify OneClickUpsell. Don't assume this slot is Plus-only by default: Shopify gates checkout UI extensions on the information/shipping/payment steps to Plus, but secondary sources converge that the post-purchase page itself isn't restricted the same way on standard plans — check the specific upsell app too, since some (e.g. AfterShip's checkout upsell) layer their own Plus-plus-Shopify-Payments requirement on top regardless of the platform rule. This slot converts several times higher than a pre-checkout upsell because the purchase decision is already made — but the published range is wide (roughly 3–25% depending on the source) because most of that spread comes from how actively the offer is managed, not from category or traffic quality: one vendor's own published split puts an actively-optimized offer around 16%, against roughly 4% for a merchant who turned it on once and never touched it again. Treat the number as evidence that this slot rewards maintenance, not as a target — measure your own thank-you-page conversion, and expect it to decay if the offer isn't revisited.
- Bundling: Package 2–3 complementary SKUs at a discount versus buying separately. The math that matters: the discount must cost less than the margin gained from moving two units in one order instead of one unit across two orders and two shipments.
- Rule: An upsell shown to everyone cannibalizes revenue you'd have gotten anyway from people who were about to buy the second item on their own. Segment by purchase history before turning an upsell on store-wide.

**Lever 3: Loyalty / Rewards Program**
- Best for: Brands where repeat purchase rate is already above roughly 15% from the flow stack. A loyalty program accelerates repeat behavior that already exists — it does not create it. Launching one before the flow stack (Step 2) is live puts a rewards layer on top of a leaky bucket.
- Mechanism: points per euro spent, tiers that unlock perks (early access, free shipping threshold, exclusive SKUs) — not just a discount by another name.
- Tool: Smile.io, Yotpo Loyalty, or LoyaltyLion — all integrate natively with Shopify and, where relevant, with Klaviyo for triggered point-balance and tier-upgrade emails.
- Health check: enrollment rate is a vanity number. Track redemption rate (points earned that actually get spent) and the repeat-purchase-rate gap between enrolled and non-enrolled cohorts. Multiple loyalty vendors report that programs with redemption sitting well under a quarter of points earned usually have a structural problem — tiers too far apart, rewards not worth the points — that no amount of program marketing fixes. Verify against your own cohort data rather than importing that number as a target.

**Lever 4: Price Architecture**
- Price is a product decision before it's a revenue lever: a good/better/best structure separates price-sensitive buyers from the segment you actually want to keep, without discounting the core SKU to do it.
- A price change is a test, not a guess — read it with the same discipline as any conversion test: 95% significance, 80% power, and a minimum of two full weeks to cover a complete weekly cycle. Reading it early because the number already looks good is the most common way a price test gets misread, even once significance is technically reached.
- Caution: raising price to "fix" a margin problem without checking what it does to the Step 1 CAC-payback math treats the symptom — if paid acquisition depends on a price point, a price change resets every acquisition number above it.

**Sequencing these levers:** subscription or bundling first if the category supports it — the mechanism compounds every month it runs. Loyalty second, once the flow stack has already produced a healthy repeat rate to accelerate. Price architecture last, and only as a proper test — changing price before the retention math (Step 1) is understood just relocates the unit-economics problem instead of solving it.
