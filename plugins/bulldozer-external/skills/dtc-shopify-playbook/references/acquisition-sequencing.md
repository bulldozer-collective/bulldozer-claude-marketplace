# Acquisition Sequencing

**Channel order for a DTC brand:**
1. **Meta first.** Visual, product-led categories (beauty, apparel, home) prospect best on a feed-native, creative-driven channel — and Meta gives the fastest read on which creative angle and product framing actually convert, which then informs everything else.
2. **Google Shopping / Search second.** Captures demand that already exists (branded search, category search) — lower risk than pure prospecting, and a useful signal check: if branded search volume isn't climbing as Meta spend grows, the top-of-funnel isn't actually building awareness.
3. **A third channel (TikTok or similar) only once creative production can sustain a genuinely native weekly cadence.** A channel that lives on format-native content dies fast on a monthly refresh — launching it before the brand can feed it is the most common reason a third channel underperforms and gets blamed on the platform instead of the input.

**Signal before structure.** Before building out campaign structure on any channel, confirm what's actually reaching the platform: is the purchase event the pixel/API reports the same one finance counts as revenue, at the same value? An account optimizing against an over- or under-reporting conversion event optimizes toward the wrong number no matter how well the campaigns are structured — check this before touching bids or audiences, not after.

**A sudden CAC or ROAS improvement on a non-Plus Shopify store is a reason to check tracking before celebrating.** Shopify retired the legacy checkout (checkout.liquid, Additional Scripts, and any script on the Thank You / Order Status pages) for every non-Plus store on August 26, 2026, with no grace period — stores that hadn't already moved their tracking to Web Pixels (Settings > Customer Events) were auto-migrated and had those legacy scripts silently dropped. A pixel or conversion tag still sitting in the old Additional Scripts field stopped firing that day without any error visible in the ad account. If the numbers on a brand that hasn't been audited since late August 2026 look unusually good, rule out broken purchase tracking before crediting the media plan.

**Judge by volume, not by the calendar.** Value-based automated bidding — Target ROAS on Google Ads (Google renamed it from "Maximize conversion value with a Target ROAS" to a standalone strategy in 2026, same underlying behavior) or the equivalent value-optimization goal on Meta — needs roughly 30–50 conversions per month per campaign before it has learned enough to be read or adjusted. Below that volume, comparing campaigns or "optimizing" is reading noise — consolidate spend into fewer campaigns rather than fragmenting it across many that will never individually reach a readable volume.

**Creative is the primary lever, not a final chapter.** A DTC account plateaus far more often from creative fatigue than from audience saturation. Budget for a real weekly (not monthly) creative refresh cadence before adding a new audience or a new channel — a new angle on an existing audience usually outperforms a new audience with the same three creatives.

**One kill rule, decided in advance.** Example: if a campaign's CAC exceeds the Step 1 payback ceiling across two full attribution windows — not two days — pause it and diagnose the creative before touching the bid strategy. Whatever the rule is, write it down before spend starts; a rule invented after the number looks bad is a rationalization, not a decision.

**Judge on the payback window, not day-one ROAS.** A repeat-purchase business's real payback isn't visible on day one — evaluate a channel against the CAC-payback window set in Step 1, not last-click day-one return. Judging a repeat-driven brand on day-one ROAS optimizes for the customers who were always going to convert immediately, not the ones the retention system was built to keep.

**Use what's already connected.** Bulldozer OS registers ad accounts for Meta, Google Ads, LinkedIn and Microsoft Ads (not TikTok or Pinterest — those two stay manual/advisory, there's no OS tool for them) and can pull spend, cost-per-conversion and creative-level performance for any of them without a manual export. That's the current numbers for the payback-window and kill-rule checks above, pulled from the connected accounts instead of re-exported from each ad platform by hand every week — but only if the date range is set explicitly: the OS analytics tool defaults to a 180-day lookback when no window is passed, which smooths the exact short-term breach the kill rule above is trying to catch. Pull the specific attribution window the kill rule names, not the tool's default range.
