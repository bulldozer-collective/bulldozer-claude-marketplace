# The 3-Operator Model and 90-Day Buildout Calendar

## The 3-Operator Model

**At seven figures, one growth generalist can run flows, campaigns, and creative at once. At eight figures, that stops being true** — the moment any one of those three needs a genuinely weekly cadence, a single owner starts trading one lever for another, and creative is usually the one that gets cut first even though Step 6 just established it's the one that matters most.

| Operator | Owns | Measured by | Native cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retention / Lifecycle** | Flow stack (Step 2), SMS (Step 3), campaign calendar (Step 4), LTV levers (Step 5) | Email + SMS as % of revenue, repeat purchase rate | Weekly campaign, flow stack reviewed monthly |
| **Acquisition / Media** | Channel sequencing, budget pacing, bid strategy, kill decisions (Step 6) | CAC vs. the Step 1 payback ceiling, spend efficiency by channel | Daily monitoring, weekly reallocation |
| **Creative** | Asset production feeding both of the above — photo, video, UGC sourcing, ad-format iteration | Output volume against the cadence Step 6 requires, creative fatigue (frequency vs. performance decay) | Weekly production |

**Hiring or assigning order:** Retention first — it's the foundation the whole playbook opens on, and it's the cheapest lever to get right because it doesn't scale with ad spend. Creative second, because it feeds both remaining functions and is the resource acquisition will be starved of if it's an afterthought. Acquisition last, and only once Step 1's gate has actually cleared — hiring a media operator before that gate clears just adds headcount pressure to spend before the business is ready to spend.

**One person, one number.** If a single person owns both a retention target and an acquisition target, the number that's easiest to see day-to-day — usually ad spend and revenue — wins their attention, and the flow stack quietly rots. That's the exact failure mode this playbook opens by warning against: brands that outbid competitors on paid before their retention math justifies the CAC.

## 90-Day Buildout Calendar

| Weeks | Build | Gate to check |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Step 1 baseline calculated. Flow 1 (Welcome) and Flow 2 (Abandoned Cart) live. | None yet — this is foundation work. |
| 3–4 | Flow 3 (Browse Abandon) live. SMS (Step 3) turned on for Welcome, Cart, and Win-Back branches. Campaigns start at the low end of cadence (6–8/month) to avoid shocking a list that's had none. | — |
| 5–6 | Priority 2 flows live: Post-Purchase (4), Repeat Purchase Prompt (5), Win-Back (6). | Check email + SMS % of revenue against the Step 2 target for the first time — too early to act on it, but the trend line starts here. |
| 7–8 | Priority 3 flows live: VIP (7), Cross-Sell (8), Subscription Lapse (9) if applicable, Repeat-Customer Post-Purchase (10). Campaign cadence at the Step 4 target (8–12/month). | Full 10-flow stack should now be live — this is the point Step 2's revenue-share target becomes a fair test. |
| 9–10 | Re-check the Step 1 unlock gate against current numbers. If cleared, begin Step 6 sequencing (Meta first) at a small, learning-phase budget — not full spend. | The gate is the decision point: paid acquisition does not start on a calendar date, it starts when the gate clears, even if that's week 14 instead of week 9. |
| 11–12 | LTV levers (Step 5): subscription or bundling first, if the category supports it. Loyalty only if repeat rate is already tracking healthy. | Compare the unit-economics scorecard (Step 1) against day-1 numbers — this is the first real read on whether the sequence is working. |
| Day 90 | Full scorecard review against Step 1 targets. Decide 3-operator staffing (Step 7) based on where time is actually going, not on a headcount plan made on day 1. | If email/SMS revenue share, repeat rate, and CAC payback all moved in the right direction, the foundation is sound enough to scale acquisition spend deliberately. If not, diagnose the flow stack before adding budget anywhere. |
