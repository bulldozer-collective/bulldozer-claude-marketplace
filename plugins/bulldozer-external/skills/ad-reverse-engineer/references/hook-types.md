# Hook Types — classifying the first 0–3 seconds

Called from Step 2 of the main framework. The hook is judged on its own, independent of
the rest of the ad — a weak hook kills an otherwise good ad before the viewer ever reaches
the body, and a strong hook can carry a mediocre one a few seconds further than it
deserves. Extract the exact opening words, the frame-1 visual, the audio (music/SFX/
silence/voice), and any text overlay before picking a type.

## The 10 types

1. **Pattern Interrupt** — visually breaks the feed with an unexpected frame, subject, or
   sound: something the thumb doesn't expect to see scrolling past identical content.
2. **Question Hook** — opens a curiosity gap, often literally a question ("Has this ever
   happened to you?"), that the rest of the ad is structured to answer.
3. **Bold Claim** — a specific, strong statement up front, usually carrying a number, a
   superlative, or an explicit comparison ("This cut my commute in half").
4. **POV Hook** — places the viewer inside a relatable scenario, frequently marked with
   "POV:" on screen, so the viewer reads themselves into the first frame.
5. **Authority/Stat Hook** — opens on credibility: data, credentials, research, or an
   expert framing, before the pitch itself starts.
6. **Visual Hook (No Words)** — carries the open on a striking or satisfying visual alone,
   no text or speech — works because the image itself stops the scroll.
7. **Controversy/Hot Take** — states a provocative or counter-consensus opinion that
   challenges what the audience already believes, to create friction fast.
8. **Social Proof Opening** — leads with reviews, ratings, or a viral reference before the
   product itself appears.
9. **Story/Narrative Opening** — starts a personal story mid-scene, with an open thread
   the viewer wants to see resolved.
10. **Direct Address/Call Out** — names the target audience explicitly in the first line
    ("If you're over 40..."), so the right viewer self-selects instantly.

## Signals of effectiveness

**Strong hooks** share these traits — check for them regardless of which type is picked:
- Stop the scroll: the first frame reads as visually distinct from whatever surrounds it
  in-feed, not as more of the same.
- Leave an open question the rest of the ad is built to close.
- Work with or without audio (most feed viewing starts muted).

**Weak hooks** share these failure modes:
- Open on a brand logo or the brand name before anything else happens.
- Use generic stock footage indistinguishable from a hundred other ads.
- Build slowly, with no payoff in the first 3 seconds.
- Leave the ad's purpose unclear — the viewer can't tell what's being sold or why they
  should keep watching.
- Look like many other ads already seen in the same category — novelty inside the
  category is most of what a hook is selling.

A hook can use the "right" type from the list above and still fail every signal in the
weak column — the type is a classification tool, not a guarantee. Score the signals
independently of the type label.
