# Common Mistakes

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating a Bulldozer OS pull as a finished section.** A value-proposition, positioning, ICP, or offer-structure read is a starting point to confirm with the client — not a fact to publish, and not something to leave unconfirmed just because it came back filled in.
- **Showing the AI's original read after the client already corrected it.** The positioning axes and note, the value-proposition summary/pricing/note, and the offer-structure verdict text each store the AI's draft next to an optional client correction — check which one is current before writing it into the document.
- **Inventing a category to avoid a head-to-head comparison.** It reads as ambition, but it's a bet almost nobody in the room can fund — default to the category customers already search for.
- **A target audience with no one excluded.** If nothing in Sections 2 and 7 names who this isn't for, the positioning hasn't actually chosen anything yet.
- **Writing the full persona template for a rubber-stamp stakeholder.** Reserve the three-part treatment for whoever can independently say yes or no; one line covers the rest.
- **Skipping straight to named software competitors.** The indirect alternative — a spreadsheet, an incumbent process, doing nothing — is usually the real competitor and the one most drafts leave thin.
- **Reading a competitor's G2 or Capterra page as customer truth.** Those reviews are solicited, not spontaneous — directional signal at best, never a fact to hand the client.
- **Treating a matching score across G2, Capterra, GetApp, or Software Advice as independent sources agreeing.** Capterra, GetApp, and Software Advice have shared one review database since long before 2026 — never independent of each other, at any point in time. G2 itself only joined that group, as owner, in February 2026, and has been merging the two sides' infrastructure (buyer intent, then vendor solicitation) ever since.
- **Listing a capability every named competitor also has as a differentiator.** That's table stakes — the cost of being considered — not a reason to choose this product over another.
- **Filling the Proof Points table with a number only the company itself has said.** A claim the client makes about itself isn't proof until an independent source could confirm it.
- **Leaving Brand Voice as a list of adjectives.** "Direct" or "warm" doesn't tell a writer what to do differently on the page — each tone word needs a rule or example that could actually be checked against a draft.
