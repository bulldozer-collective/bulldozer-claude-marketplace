# Product page testing — full detail (Phase 6)

Called from `SKILL.md` Phase 6. Two distinct mechanisms, gated differently — full reasoning below.

## Custom Product Pages / Custom Store Listings

- **Apple Custom Product Pages (CPP)**: up to 70 per app, each can carry up to 3 localized versions (a version counts once against the 70 regardless of its locales). Reached only through a unique URL — never found via organic search or browse — so they route a specific ad campaign or keyword intent to a screenshot/description variant built for that audience. (Apple Developer, App Store Connect Help, "Configure multiple product page versions" — checked 2026-08.)
- **Google Play Custom Store Listings (CSL)**: capped at 50 per app (100 for Play Partner Program members), covering all listing types combined — by country, by keyword, by campaign, by referral URL, by install state. Same purpose as Apple's CPPs: a tailored listing per acquisition channel or segment, reached the same way — through a dedicated link, not organic discovery. (Google Play Console, "Custom store listings" — checked 2026-09; the 50/100 figures are not confirmed by a directly-read official page — support.google.com and the Play Console help page blocked every attempt — but are stable across multiple independent specialist-ASO sources and consistent with the skill's own figure since 2026-08-30.)

Flag this half of the phase only when the audit finds active paid campaigns, QR codes, or other referral links pointing at the store listing — building a CPP or CSL with no channel to route to it is work with nothing to point at it. Apple provides no native statistics engine for a CPP's performance: reading "does this variant beat the default" is on you, so apply the same bar as any other conversion test you instrument yourself — 95% significance, 80% power, and a minimum two-week window to absorb a full weekly cycle before shifting traffic to a variant (see `ab-testing` for the full method). A CPP or CSL read after three good days is exactly the kind of early read that gets undone the following week.

## Apple Product Page Optimization (PPO)

PPO is Apple's native A/B-testing feature for the **default** product page — not a Custom Product Page, and the two don't overlap: Apple's own documentation confirms PPO tests aren't available for CPPs. Up to 3 treatments test variations of screenshots, app preview video, and icon against the current default, drawing from **every** traffic source that lands on the listing — organic search, browse, referral, and paid alike — not just paid installs. That means PPO is worth flagging for any app with enough listing traffic to clear the read threshold below, whether or not it runs any paid acquisition at all; it isn't gated behind "does this app buy installs" the way CPP/CSL work is.

**Reading a PPO test is not the generic conversion-test bar.** Apple runs its own Bayesian engine on the results: a treatment gets labeled "Performing Better" or "Performing Worse" once it reaches **90% confidence** — not the 95%/80% bar used above for a self-instrumented CPP/CSL comparison — and nothing shows in Analytics until the test has at least **5 first-time downloads** attributed to it. There's no fixed minimum duration: the test window runs up to 90 days, and a larger effect resolves with less data than a small one — a test can legitimately call a winner in under two weeks if the lift is big enough, and one that's still "Likely to be Inconclusive" near 90 days probably won't resolve. Applying the CPP's two-week floor to a PPO test either stops it before Apple's own engine has enough downloads to speak, or holds it open past the point Apple already called it. (Apple Developer, App Store Connect Analytics Help, "Product Page Optimization" — checked 2026-09, direct read.)

## Android equivalent of PPO — not established

Google Play Console has its own native listing test tool ("Store Listing Experiments"), the apparent Android equivalent of PPO. Its statistical-confidence mechanism and test duration have not been confirmed at an official source despite repeated attempts (support.google.com and play.google.com/console pages blocked on every try) — specialist-ASO aggregators disagree with each other on the default confidence threshold (some say 90%, some 95%+), which is itself a sign of recycled, unaudited content rather than a real reading of the source page. Nothing about Store Listing Experiments is asserted in this skill; if a future pass gets a readable official page, this is the direct Android parallel to the PPO position above and should be added the same way.
