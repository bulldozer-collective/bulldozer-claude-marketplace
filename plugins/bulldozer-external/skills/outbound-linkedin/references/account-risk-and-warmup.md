# Prospect-Reading Risk and Account Warm-Up

## Reading a Prospect Is a Different Risk Than Contacting Them

Two of the OS mechanisms above look similar but carry different risk profiles, and treating them as one thing is a mistake a generic playbook makes. `bdzCreateLinkedInPostListJob`, `bdzCreateLinkedInEmployeeListJob`, and `bdzCreateLinkedInRefreshConfig` **read** public data — posts, comments, reactions, employee lists. `LEMLIST_STEP_TYPE_LINKEDIN_INVITE` and `LEMLIST_STEP_TYPE_LINKEDIN_SEND` **act** on the platform — a connection request or a message landing in someone's inbox. Converging 2026 sources (all LinkedIn-automation vendors, so read with that bias in mind, but consistent with each other on this specific point) treat these as two separate risk profiles: LinkedIn's detection is reported to key off the *pattern* of the action — identical delays, near-instant sends, the same message text going out at volume — more than off a raw scrape-volume number, and outbound-facing automation (invites, messages) is described as more closely watched than read-only access to already-public data. Practically: scan and qualify broadly through the OS's read-side tools without worrying much about it, but keep the actual sends — Touches 1, 2, 4 — inside the per-account volume ceilings in Non-Negotiables above, whether those sends happen by hand or through a Lemlist step. Automating the reading is low-risk; automating the asking is what needs the volume discipline.

## Warm Up a New or Dormant LinkedIn Account Before Full Cadence

An account that's brand new, or has gone quiet for a while, does not start at the volume ceilings in Non-Negotiables — the same logic as warming up a sending domain before a cold email blast. Converging 2026 sources agree on the direction (ramp from a low starting volume up to full cadence over several weeks, favor ordinary human-looking activity — visits, follows, a few organic connections — before adding automated outreach on top) but disagree sharply on the exact numbers (weekly step-ups cited range from roughly 5–10/day in week one up to 30–40/day by week four, with some sources instead citing 6–8 weeks flat for a brand-new account). Rechecked 2026-10-10: a separate search on the steady-state weekly ceiling (not the ramp itself) puts a new or recently-restricted account at roughly ~50 connection requests/week once past the first push — consistent in order of magnitude with the ~5–10/day week-one figure already here, not a contradiction, but still a different source and not cross-checked against it directly. None of these sources is an independent authority the way Google Postmaster Tools is for email deliverability — they're all vendors of LinkedIn automation tools — so treat this as a real practice to follow, not a numeric target to hit. `LEMLIST_STEP_TYPE_LINKEDIN_VISIT` and `_FOLLOW` are the OS's mechanism for that lighter pre-touch activity if the account needs to build up some history before Touch 1 starts landing.
