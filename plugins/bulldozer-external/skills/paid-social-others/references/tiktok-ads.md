# TikTok Ads — Targeting, Creative, Budget

### TikTok Targeting for B2B

TikTok has no job title targeting. Build ICP audiences through behavioral proxies:

**New, coarser B2B interest categories (test as a layer, don't rely on it alone):** several 2026 agency guides report TikTok now surfaces broad B2B-flavored interest categories (enterprise software, IT solutions, business services) alongside its consumer taxonomy. This is nowhere near LinkedIn's job-title precision, and it isn't confirmed on TikTok's own targeting documentation (blocked this session, same as always) — stack it under the behavioral targeting below rather than treating it as a solved job-title substitute.

**Interest layer stacking (B2B SaaS example — targeting marketing leads):**
"Entrepreneurship" + "Marketing" + "Technology" + "Business News" = approximates marketing professionals at growth companies

For buying authority signals, add: "Investing" + "Finance" — this skews toward people with budget decision authority.

**Lookalike audiences:** Upload your customer email list. Start with 1% similarity for tightest match. Expand to 2–3% once you've validated conversion rates. Lookalikes from paying customers outperform lookalikes from trial starters.

**Behavioral targeting over interest:** Users who recently searched for terms in your category or watched competitor review content show active intent signals — prioritize behavioral targeting before broadening to interest.

**Retargeting layer:** 75%+ video viewers, pricing page visitors, trial page visitors. This is where TikTok's cost efficiency compounds — first-touch awareness from broad targeting, conversion from precise retargeting.

### TikTok Smart+: When to Hand Over, When Not To

TikTok's automated campaign type (Smart+) now lets advertisers automate targeting, bidding, placement, and creative selection together, with module-level control to keep some of those manual and automate the rest. It genuinely runs cheaper on average than a manual build — but it needs volume it won't have on a fresh B2B test: the consistent number across current TikTok guidance is roughly 50 conversions a week to complete its learning phase, with a starting budget sized to reach that (a rough multiple of your target CPA, not a flat minimum). Below that, Smart+ is optimizing on noise, same as any Smart Bidding strategy under-fed elsewhere. It also can't do what the manual interest-stacking above does: find a genuinely niche B2B audience like "marketing directors at growth-stage SaaS companies" — hand it discovery only once the account has enough conversions banked from the manual build in this section, and keep manual targeting for any segment TikTok's own interest categories can't approximate.

### TikTok Creative Rules

**The non-negotiable rule: the creative must look like organic content.** Polished brand spots get punished by the algorithm. If your video was produced by an agency for YouTube, it will not work on TikTok. The benchmark is: if this posted organically, would it get engagement?

**Creative framework for B2B SaaS:**
```
Hook (0–3 seconds): State the problem or share a surprising fact — "If your team is still doing X manually..."
Problem agitation (3–15 seconds): Show or describe the pain they recognize
Solution reveal (15–30 seconds): Introduce the product as the answer — show the product in use, not a logo
Offer (final 3–5 seconds): Low-commitment CTA — "grab our free template" or "comment GUIDE for the playbook" rather than "book a demo"
```

**Spark Ads (most effective format for B2B):** Boost an existing organic post or creator-made content. Spark Ads maintain the original engagement count (likes, comments), which builds social proof. Spark Ads with genuine engagement outperform cold creative by significant margin — use them whenever an organic piece resonates.

**Native feeling signals:**
- Vertical video only (9:16)
- No logo in first 3 seconds
- Captions on every video (80%+ of TikTok is viewed silent)
- Creator voice or face-to-camera beats polished brand video
- Trending audio where appropriate — check TikTok Creative Center weekly

**What kills TikTok B2B performance:**
- Repurposing horizontal LinkedIn or YouTube creative
- Starting with a logo or brand name
- Corporate voiceover
- Hard sell CTAs in the first 15 seconds

### The Learning-Phase Trap

TikTok's own Ads Manager help center is explicit that a "significant edit" — to budget, audience, bid, or creative — restarts an ad group's learning phase. That's not agency folklore, it's the platform's documented mechanic, and it's why the checkpoint structure below only allows one kind of mid-flight edit: swapping creative that fails the day-15 hook-rate check. Touching budget or bid because day-20 CPL looks high doesn't fix the CPL — it resets the algorithm's calibration and produces a second, noisier learning phase inside the same 45-day window, which is the single fastest way to turn a slow-starting test into a genuinely broken one. If early numbers are worrying, wait for the day-45 checkpoint rather than editing.

### TikTok Budget and Benchmarks

Minimum test budget: €3,000–5,000/month (TikTok requires minimum €50/day per ad group)
Learning phase: 45 days (TikTok's algorithm needs more volume than Reddit)

| Metric | Target | Warning | Stop |
|--------|--------|---------|------|
| Hook rate (3-sec view ÷ impressions) | >30% | 20–30% | <20% |
| Video completion rate | >25% | 15–25% | <15% |
| CTR | >1.0% | 0.5–1.0% | <0.5% |

**45-day checkpoint, 60-day go/no-go:** at day 45, judge hook rate and completion rate only — these tell you if the creative works before you've spent enough to judge cost efficiency. If hook rate is below the warning band at day 45, the creative is wrong; swap it before spending another cent on media, don't wait for day 60 to fix a creative problem. At day 60, judge CPL against your Reddit or LinkedIn CPL at equal lead quality (same SQL conversion rate check as Reddit). TikTok CPLs run lower than LinkedIn's but the lead quality gap is wider than on Reddit — don't scale on volume alone.
