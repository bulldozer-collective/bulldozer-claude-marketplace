# Pinterest Ads — Targeting, Creative, Budget

### Pinterest Targeting

Pinterest targeting is built around **interests, keywords, and boards**, closer to search intent than social discovery:

- **Keyword targeting:** bid on terms users type into Pinterest search (closer to Google intent than to TikTok/Meta interest targeting) — e.g., "productivity templates," "small business tools," "freelancer workflow."
- **Interest targeting:** broad categories (Entrepreneurship, Small Business, Productivity) — wide reach, low precision, best for top-of-funnel awareness only.
- **Actalike audiences** (Pinterest's lookalike): built from your customer list or site-visitor pixel data; narrower percentages match more tightly, same trade-off as TikTok's lookalikes.
- **Retargeting:** pixel-based, site visitors and pin engagers — this is where most B2B-adjacent Pinterest spend should sit, since cold Pinterest traffic for software has weak purchase intent.

### Pinterest Performance+: Automated Campaigns

Pinterest now positions Performance+ — automated bidding, budgeting, targeting, and creative optimization combined — as its default campaign type rather than an alternative to manual setup. It's built for what Pinterest actually is good at: a product catalog, accurate conversion data feeding it, and enough volume for the algorithm to find a pattern. None of that describes a B2B SaaS test running the manual, low-volume setup above. Don't default into Performance+ for the experimental Pinterest test this skill describes — it needs the catalog and conversion volume of an e-commerce account to earn its keep, and a B2B SaaS test without either is better served by the manual keyword/interest targeting above, where you can actually see what did or didn't work. Pinterest keeps adding to Performance+ rather than away from it (a Priority Products control and an at-a-glance campaign optimization score both landed around its September 2026 advertiser event) — none of it changes the calculus here: it's still built for catalog-scale e-commerce.

### Pinterest Creative Rules

**Static, vertical, and useful — Pinterest rewards pins that look like something a user would save even without the ad wrapper.** A pin that reads as an advertisement gets scrolled past; a pin that reads as a checklist, a template preview, or a "before/after" gets saved and re-surfaced by the algorithm for weeks after the campaign ends — that longevity is unique to Pinterest among the platforms in this skill.

**Format guidance:**
- 2:3 vertical aspect ratio (Pinterest's native format — horizontal creative underperforms sharply)
- Text overlay stating the value prop directly on the image — Pinterest users skim, they don't watch
- Idea Pins (multi-page, video-capable) for anything that benefits from a short "how it works" sequence

**What kills Pinterest performance for a software product:**
- Screenshots of a UI with no context — nothing to save or aspire to
- Stock photography with no text overlay
- Treating it like a smaller, cheaper Meta feed

### Pinterest Budget and Benchmarks

Minimum test budget: €1,500–2,500/month — the smallest test of the three platforms in this skill, appropriate to its experimental status for a B2B ICP.
Learning phase: 30 days, but expect a longer tail: pins keep generating impressions well after the campaign period because of Pinterest's search-like re-surfacing.

| Metric | Target | Warning | Stop |
|--------|--------|---------|------|
| CTR | >0.5% | 0.3–0.5% | <0.3% |
| Save rate (saves ÷ impressions) | >0.5% | 0.2–0.5% | <0.2% |
| CPC | <€2.00 | €2–4 | >€4 |

**30-day go/no-go:** if save rate is at or above target but CTR and CPC are weak, the creative is landing but the offer or landing page isn't converting search-intent traffic — fix the destination before cutting the platform. If save rate is below the stop threshold too, the format itself is wrong for the ICP: this is the signal to stop testing Pinterest for this product, not to iterate further.

**What earns Pinterest a bigger budget, and what doesn't.** A save rate that clears target for two consecutive 30-day cycles — not one — is the only signal worth escalating spend on. Pinterest's re-surfacing means a single strong month can be one pin catching on, not a repeatable pattern; don't scale Pinterest budget off one good checkpoint the way you might for Reddit or TikTok, where the signal is more tightly tied to that period's own spend.
