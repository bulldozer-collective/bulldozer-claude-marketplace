# Diagnosing a Traffic Drop Before the Audit

- **Shape of the drop first.** A sudden, overnight or 24–72h drop across many pages at once points to a manual action, a deindexation event, a technical break (bad deploy, robots/noindex pushed live, a canonical change), or a confirmed Google algorithm update — check [Google's Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com) for an open incident or a recent update rollout before auditing anything else; it's the fastest way to tell "everyone got hit" from "something on this site broke." A gradual decline over weeks looks different: content decay, a competitor displacing the site term by term, or slow crawl/technical erosion — that's the case where the five-point sequence above actually earns its keep.
- **Clicks holding steady while position holds or improves is not an SEO problem.** AI Overviews and other SERP features compress click-through even at unchanged rank — multiple trackers converge around a 50–65% CTR drop for queries where an AI Overview triggers, concentrated on informational/upper-funnel intent more than transactional. Read clicks and position together before diagnosing a page: if position is flat or better and clicks fell, the SERP is capturing the click, not the page losing relevance — rewriting the page for "better ranking" fixes nothing and wastes the client's dev budget. Distribution problem, not a visibility problem.
- **Pull the daily series, not a spot-check.** `bdzGetGscDomainAnalyticsSummary` (rather than paging through `bdzGetGscDomainAnalytics`) returns `summary.daily` — a clicks/impressions/position series across the window — in one call, and its position figure is impression-weighted, not an arithmetic mean of daily positions. Averaging daily position values by hand overweights low-traffic days and can hide or invent a trend that isn't there; use the tool's own aggregation. The daily series is also how you tell a cliff from a slope when the client's own description of "dropped last month" is vague.
- **Check the GSC reliability caveats under Indexation below before trusting any number in this diagnosis** — the logging bug window and the two reporting-delay incidents apply directly to a traffic-drop read, and a "drop" that lands inside the logging-bug window (13 May 2025 – 27 April 2026) may partly be a data artifact, not a real decline.
