# Pre-Event Detail — Go/No-Go Number, Tier 1 Sizing, Outreach Sequence, Booth Decisions

## Event Strategy Rationale — go/no-go number

**Before running the full six-week program on a pipeline-generation goal, name the number that would tell you to stop.** If the team can't state its next-best channel's cost per opportunity — outbound, paid, whatever it already runs — there's no benchmark to judge this event against, and building the whole pre-event outreach machine on a first-time or unproven event is a bet dressed up as a plan. On a new or unproven event: run the lighter Tier 2 cadence only, skip the dedicated one-pager and the T-6-week research pass, and measure it through the ROI Measurement Framework below before committing the full program next year. The event itself is never the alternative worth comparing against — the real alternative is the same budget spent on the channel already running.

## T-6 Weeks — sizing Tier 1

4. Size Tier 1 to what the attending team can actually run a full 4-touch, per-account-researched sequence for — not a fixed round number. Fifty Tier 1 accounts for a 3-person team means roughly 17 fully-researched relationships per rep on top of their existing pipeline in four weeks; the same 50 for a solo rep is a wish, not a plan. Do the division against your own headcount before naming a number. Tier 2 (the lighter 2-touch sequence below, no per-account research) can run roughly double Tier 1's count per rep, since it costs less time per account.

## T-4 Weeks: Pre-Event Outreach (Tier 1)

**The goal is to book meetings before the event, not at the event.** A meeting confirmed on the calendar before you land converts at a fraction of the effort a badge scan does after the fact — the prospect already agreed to the conversation, instead of you reconstructing interest from a business card three days later. Teams that arrive with a full pre-booked calendar consistently outperform teams relying on badge swipes and impromptu conversations; treat the exact multiplier as unmeasured until you track your own event's numbers (see ROI Measurement Framework below), not as a benchmark to quote to a client.

**Pre-event outreach sequence (for Tier 1 accounts):**

Touch 1 (T-4 weeks, LinkedIn connection request):
```
"[Name] — saw you're speaking at/attending [Event]. I'll be there with [team] — would love to connect ahead of time if you're open to it."
```
Note: keep it under 300 characters — that's the connection-note field's outer limit on Premium, Sales Navigator, and Recruiter. Public sources disagree on whether a free/basic account gets the same 300 or a shorter cap (200 shows up in some LinkedIn help content, unconfirmed here) — write the ask into the first sentence so the note survives being cut short either way, and check the live character counter in the compose box rather than assuming the number. No pitch. No CTA beyond connecting. Free-plan accounts are also capped at a handful of personalized invites per month before LinkedIn drops the note option entirely — check the sender's plan before promising a note on every Tier 1 send.

Touch 2 (T-3 weeks, LinkedIn DM after acceptance):
```
"[Name] — looking forward to [Event]. [One sentence on what you noticed about their work — their talk topic, a recent post, a company milestone]. We help teams like yours [specific outcome]. Would it make sense to grab 20 minutes at the conference — I can come to you?"
```

Touch 3 (T-2 weeks, email):
Subject: "Meeting at [Event]?"
```
[Name] — reaching out separately from LinkedIn.

We'll have [X people] at [Event] focused on [specific problem you solve]. Given [company]'s work on [specific thing you noticed], I think there's something worth a 20-minute conversation.

Happy to make it work around your schedule — are you free [specific morning/afternoon block]?
```

Touch 4 (T-1 week, calendar slot):
If they've replied positively but haven't confirmed a time: send a specific calendar slot. Don't ask "when are you free?" — propose a specific time and let them accept or offer an alternative.

**Tier 2 outreach:** Simplified 2-touch sequence (LinkedIn + email). Goal: warm introduction and awareness. Meeting at the event is a nice-to-have, not the primary objective.

## T-2 Weeks: On-Site Preparation — booth decision

**Decide whether to staff a booth — this is a staffing conflict, not a budget line.** A booth needs someone at it for the full show-floor hours, or it reads as abandoned. For a 2–3 person team running the Tier 1 meeting-first model in parallel, that's a real conflict: booth hours and Tier 1 meeting hours are the same hours. Two options, not a third — staff the booth with people who are *not* running Tier 1 meetings (a dedicated SDR, a hired booth staffer), or skip the booth and attend as delegates running the meeting-first model exclusively. A small team trying to do both ends up half-staffing the booth and arriving late to meetings.

**Refuse a booth strategy built around a raffle or swag giveaway to drive scans.** It inflates the "attendees engaged" count in the funnel below without producing a single qualified conversation, and it trains the team to report scan volume instead of the qualification tier that actually predicts pipeline. If the booth needs a reason for someone to stop, that reason is a specific question, a live demo, or the one-pager — not a prize wheel.
