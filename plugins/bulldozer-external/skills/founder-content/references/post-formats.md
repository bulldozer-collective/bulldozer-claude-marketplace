# Post Structure — Hooks and Formats

## Hook examples

- A bold opinion stated as fact: "Most founders kill their pipeline with LinkedIn. Here's what they're doing wrong."
- A counterintuitive observation: "We raised our prices 3x and our close rate went up."
- A specific number that raises a question: "12 months ago I had 800 followers. Here's what changed."
- A relatable failure: "I posted for 8 months with nothing to show for it. Month 9 changed everything."

## Post body formats

*Story post* (Pillar 2): Hook → context → conflict → resolution → lesson. End with a question that invites the ICP to share their version.

*Insight post* (Pillar 1): Hook → the insight stated plainly → 3 supporting reasons → the implication for the reader. No bullet points that could have been written by an AI. Each reason must be specific enough that it couldn't apply to a different category.

**When a Pillar 1 insight is a framework, checklist, or step-by-step — build it as a document post instead of a text post.** `bdzCreateLinkedInPost` takes a `documentUrl` (PDF, PPT, PPTX, DOC or DOCX, up to 100MB and 300 pages) to publish a document post — LinkedIn's own "Carousel" ad unit is sponsored-only, this is the organic equivalent, and it carries the format's own `documentTitle`. A named, dated study (Socialinsider, ~1.3M posts across ~16,600 business pages, January 2024–December 2025) puts document posts at the highest average engagement of any format on the platform — 7.00%, still rising (+14% year-over-year) — well ahead of a text post carrying the same idea as a formatted list. Reserve this for Pillar 1: a Pillar 2 story rarely gains from being sliced into slides, and forcing one into a deck just to chase the format usually reads as filler.

*Contrarian post* (Pillar 3): Hook → the conventional wisdom stated as the enemy → why it's wrong → the alternative position → the evidence. Expect pushback. Respond to comments in the first hour after publishing — LinkedIn's ranking tests a post's early engagement before deciding how far to push it, and a comment (especially one that starts a back-and-forth) carries more weight than a reaction.
