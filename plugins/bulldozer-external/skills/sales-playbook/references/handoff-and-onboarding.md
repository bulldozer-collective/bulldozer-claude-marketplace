# CS Handoff Protocol and Rep Onboarding Path

## Step 7: CS Handoff Protocol

A handoff that transfers only the contract loses everything that made the deal winnable: why the customer bought, what they were promised, and who actually cares about the outcome. Handoff is a short process with a deadline, not a single document dropped in a shared folder.

**At contract signature, the rep completes a handoff record with:**
- **Why they bought** — the specific pain and quantified impact from discovery, in the customer's words, not a generic summary
- **Success criteria** — what the customer will consider a win, and by when (this is what CS is actually accountable for delivering)
- **Commitments made during the sale** — anything promised verbally or in the proposal: a custom integration, a timeline, a specific feature, a pricing exception. Undocumented commitments become CS's problem the moment the customer mentions them in onboarding
- **Stakeholder map** — economic buyer, champion, day-to-day users, and anyone who can still block or stall the account
- **Risks** — anything that could threaten renewal: an internal champion who might leave, a competing tool still in place, a stakeholder who was never fully bought in

**Timeline:**
1. Handoff record completed in CRM the day the contract is signed — not left for end of week
2. CS kickoff scheduled within 48 hours of signature
3. A live handoff conversation between rep and CS owner before the customer kickoff call — covering tone, politics, and anything too sensitive to put in writing, not just a re-read of the CRM fields
4. Rep joins the first customer kickoff call to transfer trust in person, then steps back

**Common failure:** the rep marks the deal Closed-Won and moves on to the next pipeline before the handoff record is complete. The deal is not done until CS has what it needs to deliver the outcome that was sold.

## Step 8: Rep Onboarding Path

New reps ramp on the playbook, not on osmosis from shadowing calls. Structure the first 90 days around graduated ownership, not a fixed training calendar.

**Days 1–30: Foundation**
- Study this playbook section by section: ICP and personas, then stage map, then discovery framework
- Shadow 5+ live discovery and demo calls with tenured reps; debrief each one against the discovery framework — what was mapped, what was missed
- Role-play the top objections from Step 5 until the responses don't sound scripted
- Certification gate before touching a live deal: run a mock discovery call and a mock demo in front of a manager or senior rep

**Days 31–60: Supervised ownership**
- Own discovery and demo calls on real deals, with a manager reviewing call recordings weekly against the MEDDPICC framework
- Shadow a tenured rep on negotiation and closing — this is the stage new reps are least ready to run alone
- Track qualification accuracy: how many deals the rep advanced past discovery actually had a real economic buyer and timeline

**Days 61–90: Full ownership**
- Run the full cycle independently, prospecting through handoff, on a reduced pipeline load
- Weekly 1:1 review of pipeline against stage exit criteria, not just activity volume
- Exit this stage only once the rep has closed at least one deal end-to-end, including a clean CS handoff

**What "ramped" means:** the rep can run a full-cycle deal without a manager reviewing every step, not that 90 days have elapsed. A rep who hits day 90 without a closed deal is not ramped — extend supervised ownership rather than declaring them ready on a calendar date.
