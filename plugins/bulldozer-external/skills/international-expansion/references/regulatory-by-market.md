# Regulatory Considerations by Market

### European Union / EEA

- **GDPR**: Applies from day 1 if you handle EU personal data (you almost certainly do). Requires: privacy policy, data processing agreements with vendors, lawful basis for processing, user consent or legitimate interest, right-to-erasure capability. *Watch item, not yet actionable*: the European Commission tabled a "Digital Omnibus" package (19 Nov 2025) that would ease record-keeping duties for SMEs and a new "small mid-cap" category; re-checked 2026-10-11, still not adopted. The sticking point remains the same across the year — a new pseudonymization article creating an actor-relative regime where the same data can count as pseudonymized (so lighter-touch) for one party and still personal for another — and the article's own number has moved as the Council cycled compromise texts: Council working documents from spring 2026 build out an "Article 29a" (general controller/processor obligations, tied to the CJEU's EDPS v SRB ruling, C-413/23), while a civil-society letter (EDRi, to member states, mid-September 2026) describes the same mechanism under "Article 25a" with accompanying Recitals 27-27c — real renumbering across rounds, not two competing drafts, and still not worth picking one number to repeat to a client. None of the day-1 obligations above are relaxed yet, and aggregated reporting still puts final adoption of this half no earlier than late 2026. Its final shape is genuinely unsettled, not just slow: a June 2026 Council compromise reportedly dropped the Commission's own proposed authority to define pseudonymization criteria in favor of expanding the European Data Protection Board's mandate instead, and business federations publicly opposed that direction the same month — a reminder that the mechanism itself, not just the timeline, is still moving. One more thing worth keeping separate in a client's head: the package also has an AI-law half (different articles, a separate legislative track) that *did* finish and was signed into law on 8 July 2026 — that's unrelated to the GDPR/data half described here, so a headline about "the Digital Omnibus being adopted" is not evidence that this record-keeping relief is in force.
- **VAT**: EU VAT rules apply to digital services sold to EU consumers. B2B sales typically handled via reverse charge, but varies by country. For B2C: a single EU-wide threshold of €10,000/year in cross-border distance sales (goods and digital services combined, net of VAT) has applied since 1 July 2021 — cross the threshold and VAT is due in each customer's country, not the seller's; a non-EU seller gets no threshold at all and is liable from the first sale. Above the threshold, the One Stop Shop (OSS) lets an EU-established seller file one quarterly return instead of registering in every destination country separately. This is corroborated by several independent VAT-compliance vendors as unchanged for 2026 (checked 2026-10-11), not read directly from an EU legal source — official EU VAT/OSS pages were not reachable from this environment (see Non traité) — but the figure, mechanism and 2021 effective date are consistent across sources for a rule that hasn't moved in five years, which is a different confidence level than a single blog's number.
- **Employment law**: Significantly more employee-protective than US. Termination requires cause and notice periods. Non-compete enforceability varies.

### United Kingdom

- **UK GDPR**: Post-Brexit, UK has its own GDPR equivalent. Similar requirements.
- **FCA regulation**: If your product touches financial services, check FCA requirements early.

### Germany (specific)

- **Works council**: If hiring >5 employees, a works council can be formed — this affects hiring, termination, and changes to working conditions.
- **Imprint requirement**: All German websites require an Impressum (legal notice with company info) — the duty moved from the old Telemediengesetz to the Digitale-Dienste-Gesetz (DDG, in force since 2024), but the obligation itself is unchanged. Missing this is a compliance risk.
- **Data hosting**: Some German enterprises require data hosted in Germany (Hetzner, Deutsche Telekom).

### France (specific)

- **Labour code**: 35-hour workweek, strong protections. Employment contracts must be in French.
- **CNIL**: French data protection authority. Similar GDPR requirements plus some French-specific rules.
- **Formal business culture**: Contracts and relationships require more formality than Anglo-Saxon markets. Expect longer sales cycles.

### Japan (specific)

- **Language**: English rarely sufficient; Japanese localization is mandatory for serious market entry.
- **Local entity**: Often required by large customers.
- **Hanko**: Physical seal used on contracts historically; digital contracts now accepted but confirm with each customer.
