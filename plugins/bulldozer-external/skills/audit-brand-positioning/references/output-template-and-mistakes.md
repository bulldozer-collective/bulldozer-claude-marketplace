## Common Mistakes

- **Reaching for a rebrand when the finding is a messaging problem.** A rebrand — new name, new visual identity, a new category claim — is a different, far more expensive intervention than a positioning fix, and it's justified only when the audit finds the company isn't solving a distinct problem for anyone. If the real story exists but was never said clearly or consistently, the fix is sharper messaging and consistency work, not a rebrand. Reserve the bigger recommendation for the bigger finding, not for how anxious the client sounds when they open the audit.
- **Treating every dimension scored ≤6 as equally urgent.** A scorecard with five gaps and no stated priority isn't a diagnosis, it's a wish list. The audit has to say which gap gets fixed this quarter and which ones the client is explicitly choosing not to touch yet — and why (tie it to the commercial signal in Step 7, not to which dimension feels most uncomfortable).
- **Building the whitespace map against named competitors only.** See Step 5 — when deal data says the real alternative is "no vendor" or the incumbent manual process, a map of named competitors alone is answering the wrong question.
- **Reading the logo swap test as one verdict.** See Step 5 — messaging differentiation and visual differentiation are separate failures with separate fixes; a single "yes, they look the same" collapses two different problems into one.
- **Taking a CRM-logged Closed Lost Reason at face value.** See Step 4 — the rep who lost the deal is the least neutral source for why, and the industry practice this audit follows (independent win-loss reads) exists specifically to correct for that.
- **Sizing the Action Plan to marketing's ambition instead of the team that has to run it.** A Week 1 / Month 1 / Quarter plan that asks a two- or three-person team to rewrite the website, retrain sales, and relaunch positioning inside one quarter isn't ambitious — it's a plan nobody executes. Sequence around the single highest-commercial-impact fix from Step 7 first, not everything the audit surfaced at once.
