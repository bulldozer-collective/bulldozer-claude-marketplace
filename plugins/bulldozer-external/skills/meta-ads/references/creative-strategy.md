# Creative Strategy

### The 3-Second Rule

If the first 3 seconds don't hook the viewer, the rest of the ad doesn't matter. Design the hook first.

**Hook types that work**:
- Bold problem statement: "Stop [common pain]"
- Unexpected visual or statistic
- Pattern interrupt (weird, surprising, contrarian)
- Direct address to ICP: "If you're a [role] who [problem]..."

### Creative Hierarchy (Test in This Order)

1. **Concept/angle** — biggest impact lever (problem-led vs. outcome-led vs. social proof-led)
2. **Hook/headline** — stop the scroll
3. **Visual style** — polished vs. UGC vs. screenshot
4. **Body copy length** — short vs. medium vs. long
5. **CTA copy** — last lever to test

Test concept first. Never test CTA copy before validating the concept works.

**Don't call a winner on CTR alone.** CTR tells you a hook stopped the scroll, not that the audience converted — an ad can out-CTR the field and still be the worst cost-per-result in the ad set. Wait for the down-funnel number (CPL/CPA) before killing or scaling, unless CTR is so low the ad clearly never got a fair look. And don't call a winner on a handful of conversions either: exiting the learning phase (see Bid Strategy) means the platform has enough signal to keep optimizing, not that you have enough sample to declare a result — treat single-digit or low-double-digit conversion counts on a given ad as noise, not a verdict. If the account's budget genuinely can't get every live variant to a real sample within the test window, that's a sign to run fewer variants at once, not to read the thin ones anyway.

### Creative Formats

| Format | When to use | Specs |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Single image | Product, offer, social-content proof | 1080x1080 (square) or 1080x1920 (vertical) |
| Carousel | Multiple products, feature list, before/after | 1080x1080 per card |
| Video (15–30 sec) | Complex products, storytelling, UGC | 1080x1920 vertical |
| Collection | E-commerce catalog | Auto-generated from product feed |

**Key insight**: Native/UGC-style content (selfie video, screen recording, text overlay) often outperforms polished studio creative. Test both — don't assume either wins.

**Advantage+ Creative runs by default — plan the copy for it, don't fight it.** Since early 2026, every new Sales/Leads/App Promotion campaign launches with all Advantage+ Creative enhancements on (text variations, visual touch-ups, animation, background generation) unless turned off ad by ad. Write primary text and headline as source material the system can recombine, not a single fixed sentence you're relying on to render verbatim. Separately, Meta now auto-labels AI-touched creative with an "AI info" tag behind the ad's three-dot menu — this fires for Advantage+ Creative's own edits and, since a mid-2026 update, for third-party AI-generated content it detects via industry-standard metadata. The advertiser carries the disclosure regardless of who configured the campaign or suggested the variant, so don't promise a client "no AI label" without checking whether Advantage+ enhancements are actually off.

### Ad Copy Framework

**Primary text** (above the image):
- Lead with the hook (problem, claim, or question)
- 1–3 short paragraphs maximum
- In Feed placements, Meta truncates behind a "See More" link around 125 characters (this is a rendering guideline tied to how many lines fit on the device, not a hard cap) — put the hook and the offer before that point
- Include social proof (customer count, review quote, stat)
- End with CTA

**Headline** (below the image):
- Value proposition in one line
- Include the offer if there is one
- Meta recommends 40 characters for full visibility; on the smallest phone screens the visible window can shrink to ~27 characters — keep the core value prop inside 27

**Description** (optional):
- Reinforcing detail or secondary benefit
