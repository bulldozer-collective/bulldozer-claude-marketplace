# IDS Protocol and Weekly KPI Template

IDS = Identify, Discuss, Solve. The structure that turns a red metric into a closed action in 15 minutes.

**Identify (3 minutes):**
State the metric, the gap, and one hypothesis for the root cause. No debate here — one hypothesis per metric owner. "Pipeline coverage is at 2.1x against 3.5x target. Hypothesis: we had 3 reps at the sales conference last week and outreach volume dropped 40%."

**Discuss (7 minutes):**
Test the hypothesis with the data available. Is the drop structural (ICP targeting) or episodic (conference week)? Pull the leading indicator: did outreach volume actually drop? Did reply rate hold up despite lower volume? Or did both drop simultaneously — which would suggest a different root cause?

The three root cause buckets for most growth metric drops:
1. **Volume problem** — Not enough top-of-funnel activity (outreach, traffic, leads). Fix: increase volume this week.
2. **Conversion problem** — Volume is fine but conversion between stages dropped. Fix: diagnose the stage where conversion dropped and identify what changed (messaging, targeting, ICP quality).
3. **Lag problem** — Nothing is actually wrong; the pipeline is building but hasn't converted yet. Fix: no action, increase monitoring.

If the same stage stays red for three or more consecutive weeks despite messaging and targeting
fixes, stop re-running IDS on it as if the next tweak will land. That persistence is usually a
positioning or ICP problem — a promise the market isn't confirming, or a segment that never fit —
not an execution problem. Escalate it as a strategy question outside the meeting rather than
letting the weekly review absorb it indefinitely. Check this at the same granularity you'd check
any comparison: a blended stage metric can look chronically red while masking one segment — a
deal-size band, a vertical, a territory — dragging the average down on its own. Pooling segments
that don't behave alike is exactly the mistake that turns a single-segment fix into a
company-wide positioning debate. Confirm the pattern holds within a segment, not only on the
blended number, before escalating it as a market-level problem.

**When more metrics are red than the block can hold:** 45 minutes ÷ 15 minutes per issue caps a
single meeting at three full IDS treatments. Don't compress the process to fit more in — a
rushed IDS produces a hypothesis with no real discussion, which is worse than not running it at
all. Triage by impact on the north star, not by order on the scorecard: the reds closest to the
NSM get the room's time. Everything else still leaves with a named owner and a same-week
check-in outside the meeting, logged in the action tracker like any other action — it does not
just carry over silently to next week's queue.

**Solve (5 minutes):**
One action, one owner, one deadline. Log it in the action tracker. The action must be specific enough that next week's meeting can verify it was done.

Format: "Who does what by when? [Name] will [specific action] by [specific date]."

Not: "The team will improve outreach quality." That's not an action.
Yes: "Alex will rewrite the top 3 outreach sequences using the updated ICP criteria and launch by Thursday."
