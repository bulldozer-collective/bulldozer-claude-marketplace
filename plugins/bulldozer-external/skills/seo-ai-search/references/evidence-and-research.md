# Evidence and Research — AI Search Statistics, Citable-Block Check and GEO Findings

## How AI Search Works — critical stats

**Critical stats** — treat all of these as directional, not audited benchmarks: every public measurement of AI Overviews uses a different query sample and methodology, so figures move by a factor of 2-3x between sources.

- AI Overviews coverage of Google searches varies widely by study, from roughly a quarter to more than half of queries depending on category and country (reported range across 2025-2026 trackers: ~25% to ~60%). Don't quote a single percentage to a client — pull their own SERP coverage if you need a number that means something for them.
- AI Overviews cut click-through to the ranking pages sharply. The 2026 evidence base is stronger than a year ago and clusters lower than earlier trackers suggested: Ahrefs' large-sample analysis (300k keywords) finds ~35% lower CTR on the top-ranking page when an AI Overview is present, and a 2026 randomized field experiment — the first *causal*, not just correlational, measurement — found a ~40% drop in outbound clicks. Pew Research's session-tracking study reports a bigger relative gap on informational queries specifically (8% of sessions click a traditional result with an AI Overview present vs. 15% without, a ~47% relative decline). Use "roughly a third to half of clicks, worse on purely informational queries" — not a single percentage, and not the older 50-65% figure still circulating in some 2025 decks.
- Brands are cited roughly 6.5x more often through third-party sources (reviews, comparison sites, industry press) than through their own domain — only a low single-digit percentage of citations point at the brand's own site (AirOps study of ChatGPT/Claude/Perplexity brand mentions, cited by Search Engine Journal).
- Citing sources and adding statistics are associated with visibility gains of **up to ~40%** in the Princeton/IIT Delhi/Georgia Tech/Allen Institute Generative Engine Optimization research (arXiv:2311.09735, 2023) — see Pillar 2 below for what's actually confirmed from that paper versus what circulates as an unverified breakdown.
- Keyword stuffing is reported by the same research to *reduce* AI visibility (opposite of classic SEO) — treat the direction as solid, the exact percentage as unverified (see Pillar 2).

## Pillar 1: Structure — the empty-block warning

**A block that passes every check above and still says nothing isn't more citable — it's emptier.** A definition block that just restates the query back as a sentence ("Attribution modeling is the process of assigning credit to marketing touchpoints") gives an AI system a clean shape to extract, but nothing worth citing over ten other pages that say the same thing. The 40-60 word passage still needs a specific claim a reader — human or model — couldn't have written themselves before formatting it into a block. Write the actual answer first; shape it for extraction second, never as a substitute for having one.

## Pillar 2: Authority — GEO research

**Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) research** — Princeton, IIT Delhi, Georgia Tech and the Allen Institute for AI (arXiv:2311.09735, 2023 — not a Princeton-alone paper, and not from Perplexity.ai or KDD as sometimes cited). Their benchmark ranked optimization methods by the visibility boost each produced. The only figure we can confirm and stand behind is the headline one:

- **Citing sources and adding statistics** produced the largest gains measured, **up to ~40%** in the paper's benchmark.
- **Keyword stuffing** was the one method that *reduced* visibility rather than raising it — direction confirmed, exact percentage not.

A more granular ranking (expert quotations, authoritative tone, clarity, technical terms, each with its own percentage) circulates widely in SEO blogs attributing it to this same study — we could not verify those individual numbers against the source (the paper's own results are shown as images we can't extract text from), so don't quote them to a client as if they were confirmed. If you need the full method-by-method breakdown, say so and treat it as reported-but-unverified rather than fact.
