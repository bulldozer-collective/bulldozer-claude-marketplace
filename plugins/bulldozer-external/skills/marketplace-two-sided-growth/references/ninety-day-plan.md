# The 90-Day Operating Plan — Detail

The plan branches on the Step 1 diagnosis. Below is the sequence for the most common cold-start case — supply too thin. If Step 1 pointed to a matching problem or a demand-side failure instead, the diagnostic table in Step 1 says which steps to run first; don't run this plan against the wrong diagnosis.

**Days 1–30 — Supply density**
- [ ] Hand-recruit toward the Step 2 range (commonly 50–100, sized to the recruiting team's real capacity) of qualified suppliers in the beachhead category, sourced by direct outreach, not paid acquisition
- [ ] Every recruited supplier clears the Step 2 onboarding checklist before counting as "active" — a profile with no pricing or availability isn't supply
- [ ] Track supply density weekly against the Step 3 gate (70%+ of searches see 3+ options). This is the number that unlocks everything downstream — not the total supplier count
- [ ] No paid demand spend in this window, on either side

**Days 31–60 — Liquidity proof**
- [ ] Generate enough matched transactions — internal test transactions plus organic and referral demand only — to measure the real liquidity ratio and time-to-match against the Step 1 targets
- [ ] Fix matching before fixing volume: a 30–50% search-to-match rate is a matching-algorithm problem, not a supply-count problem (Step 1 diagnostic table). Resolve it before adding demand, or added demand just meets the same broken match rate at higher volume
- [ ] Calibrate take rate against this cohort's actual contribution margin (Step 4). This is the cheapest point to change it — suppliers who onboard at a rate accept it; a later increase meets resistance from suppliers who've already built a client base on the platform

**Days 61–90 — Controlled demand unlock**
- [ ] Re-check every Step 3 demand-unlock gate against trailing 30-day data, not the best week in the window
- [ ] If every gate clears: open one demand channel first — referral or SEO, not paid (Step 3 channel table) — and hold paid acquisition until the new demand doesn't collapse the liquidity ratio it took 60 days to build
- [ ] If any gate is still short: stay in the Days 31–60 discipline. A missed gate is a reason to extend the phase, not a reason to start demand spend on an optimistic trend line

**Exit criteria:** every Step 3 gate cleared on trailing 30-day data, and the first demand channel live without the liquidity ratio dropping below the Step 1 flywheel threshold. If liquidity drops when demand opens, pause the channel and go back to supply — that failure mode, clearing the gates on paper and then breaking the flywheel on the first real demand push, is the most common way this plan fails in its final week.
