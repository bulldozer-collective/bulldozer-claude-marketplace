# Take Rate Calibration — Detail

## Take rate benchmarks and the subscription hybrid

**Take rate benchmarks by B2B marketplace type:**

| Marketplace type | Typical take rate | Sustainable range |
|-----------------|------------------|------------------|
| Professional services (fractional execs, consulting) | 15–25% of first placement | 15–30% |
| Vendor procurement (B2B products) | 5–12% of transaction | 3–15% |
| SaaS / software distribution | 10–30% revenue share | 10–30% |
| Staffing / talent (hourly) | 20–35% of hourly rate | 15–40% |

**Read this table as illustrative ranges, not a benchmark to defend in a board deck** —
no audited, named study publishes take rates by marketplace category, and the public data
points that do exist (a listed company disclosing its own rate) belong to one specific
business model and don't transport to another. Calibrate the rate against this
marketplace's own contribution margin below, and use the ranges above only as a sanity
check that the number isn't wildly outside where comparable platforms tend to land.

**Take rate vs. subscription hybrid:**
Many B2B marketplaces layer a subscription fee on top of transaction fees:
- Subscription (supplier): ensures suppliers are committed to the platform; covers platform overhead regardless of transaction volume
- Transaction fee: aligns platform incentive with supplier success; scales with supplier GMV
- Most durable model: subscription + reduced transaction fee (15% base → 10% after hitting monthly subscription)

## Rate-change refusals

**Refuse to raise the take rate on suppliers who already onboarded at a lower one.** A rate increase applied retroactively to a supplier who has already built a client base on the platform is the single fastest way to teach that supplier to plan an exit to a direct relationship — this is exactly the disintermediation trigger Step 5 warns about, self-inflicted. If the calibrated rate needs to move, grandfather every supplier already active at the old rate and apply the new one to net-new suppliers only. This costs short-term revenue on the existing cohort; the alternative costs the cohort itself.

**The threshold for changing your mind on the rate isn't the number, it's who's churning.** A rate that produces negative contribution margin only on a specific supplier segment (a category, a price band, a geography) is telling you that segment doesn't fit the model — kill or re-price that segment, don't raise the platform-wide rate to cover it. Only raise the rate across the board when the negative margin shows up broadly, across segments that are each individually healthy.
