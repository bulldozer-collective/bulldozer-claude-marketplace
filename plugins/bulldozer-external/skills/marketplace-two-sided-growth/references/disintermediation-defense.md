# Disintermediation Defense — Detail

**Disintermediation signals to monitor:**
- Communication moving off-platform (buyers/suppliers asking for direct contact details in early messages)
- Transaction volume growth slowing for repeat pairs (matched parties transacting less frequently on-platform)
- Supplier churn after 6–12 months despite high buyer demand
- Buyers contacting suppliers they met on-platform by email or LinkedIn

**Disintermediation defense playbook:**

**Layer 1: Payment protection**
Process all transactions through the platform. Buyers get payment protection (supplier doesn't get paid until delivery is confirmed). Suppliers get payment guarantee (no chasing invoices). Both sides have a financial reason to stay on-platform.

**Routing payment through the platform is a tax-reporting decision, not just a trust one.** In the US, a marketplace that settles supplier payouts is a third-party settlement organization for Form 1099-K purposes. The reporting trigger is currently the pre-2021 rule — more than $20,000 in gross payments AND more than 200 transactions per supplier per year — after the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) repealed the phased-down $600 threshold that had been scheduled to take effect, retroactively to tax year 2022. This is a separate mechanism from the 1099-NEC threshold that covers paying an individual directly (a referral or affiliate payout elsewhere in this playbook family) — don't conflate the two when a platform runs both a supplier-payment rail and an incentive program. Collect a W-9 (US suppliers) or W-8BEN/W-8BEN-E (non-US) before the first payout regardless of the threshold — waiting until a supplier crosses it means chasing the form after the money has already moved.

**Payment protection is weaker leverage than it looks for a high-ticket, front-loaded fee.** Where the platform's entire value is captured in one event — a placement fee for a fractional CFO, a finder's fee for a one-off deal — payment protection only defends the *first* transaction. The second and third engagements between the same buyer and supplier happen off the clock the platform was ever protecting, because there's no ongoing transaction stream left to route through it. For this shape of marketplace (the fractional-executive example this playbook opens with is exactly this shape), don't lead the defense with payment protection: lead with Layer 3 (workflow integration — the relationship history, the scoping documents, the performance record) and Layer 4 (value-added services), because those are the only layers that still produce value on engagement two.

**Layer 2: Trust infrastructure**
Reviews, ratings, dispute resolution, and credentials verification make the platform the authoritative source of supplier quality. A supplier with 47 five-star reviews on the platform can't replicate that signal in a direct relationship.

**Layer 3: Workflow integration**
The deeper the platform is embedded in the transaction workflow — communication, project management, contract signing, invoicing — the higher the switching cost. A buyer and supplier communicating through the platform's messaging system can't easily take that history off-platform.

**Layer 4: Value-added services**
Insurance, compliance, background checks, certifications, and payment financing are services that buyers and suppliers can't replicate in a direct relationship. Add these behind the core match — priced as add-ons, not bundled into the take rate — so the platform's value is visibly larger than "we introduced you two." A marketplace that only ever provided the introduction has nothing left to defend once the introduction has already happened.

**Layer 5: Detection and enforcement**
Behavioral monitoring on in-platform messaging — contact-detail patterns (phone numbers, emails, "let's talk on WhatsApp"), requests to move a conversation off-platform — flags disintermediation attempts before the relationship actually leaves. Detection only works paired with a stated consequence: a policy suppliers agree to at onboarding (warning → reduced search visibility → removal) gives the platform standing to act when monitoring fires. A warning with no enforcement behind it teaches suppliers that circumvention is free.

**Watch repeat pairs specifically.** Disintermediation risk concentrates after a buyer and supplier have transacted 3+ times — track transaction-volume growth for repeat pairs separately from new-pair volume. A repeat pair whose on-platform volume is flattening while the relationship is clearly still active is the earliest reliable signal, well before a support ticket or a churned account confirms it.
