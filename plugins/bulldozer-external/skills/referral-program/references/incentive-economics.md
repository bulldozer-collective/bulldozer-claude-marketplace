# Incentive economics — cash vs. credit, and sizing by product type

## Cash vs. Credit — Not Just a Preference

The reward *type*, not just the amount, is a decision with consequences beyond marketing:

- **Product credit** stays inside your own P&L, doesn't need payout infrastructure, and reads as goodwill rather than compensation.
- **Cash** needs real payments ops, and in the US, paying a referrer cash above $2,000 in a calendar year (the 1099-NEC reporting threshold for payments made from January 1, 2026 onward) means collecting a W-9 before the first payout — the same obligation that applies to affiliate commissions (see `affiliate-program`). This is a payments and compliance decision, not just a marketing one, and it applies per referrer per year, not per program.
- **For B2B referrers who are employees, not the buyer** — a personal cash reward or gift card can read as a kickback against their own employer's gift or anti-corruption policy, especially selling into a regulated buyer (healthcare, finance, public sector). Company credit, a charitable-donation option, or a co-marketing benefit is the safer default there; save personal cash for self-serve, individual-buyer products where the referrer and the decision-maker are the same person.

## Incentive Sizing by Product Type

| Product type | Referrer reward | Referred reward |
|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| Self-serve SaaS (<$50/mo) | 1 month free or $10-$50 credit | 1 month free or $10-$30 credit |
| Mid-market SaaS ($100-$500/mo) | $100-$500 credit or cash | 20-30% off first 3 months |
| High-ACV SaaS (>$1k/mo) | % of first year revenue ($500-$2k) | Custom negotiated |
| E-commerce | 10-20% commission | 10-15% off first order |

Whatever the number, the ceiling is what the same customer costs through your next-best paid channel — if referral CAC (reward cost ÷ referred customers acquired) creeps past blended paid CAC, that's the signal to cut the reward, not to keep running it on the strength of the warm-lead story.
