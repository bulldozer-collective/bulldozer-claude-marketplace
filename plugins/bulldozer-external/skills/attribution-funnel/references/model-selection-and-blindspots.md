# Model Selection Notes and Blind Spots

**Where this runs in Bulldozer OS:** every model above is a parameter, not a manual split to compute in a spreadsheet. HubSpot's `get_campaign_attribution_reports` takes an `attributionModel` field with exactly these values — `TIME_DECAY`, `U_SHAPED`, `W_SHAPED`, `FIRST_INTERACTION`, `LAST_INTERACTION`, `FULL_PATH`, `LINEAR` (the default), `J_SHAPED`, `INVERSE_J_SHAPED` — grouped by up to two dimensions at once, from a list that includes `UTM_SOURCE`, `UTM_MEDIUM`, `UTM_CAMPAIGN` and `DEAL`. Asking for `U_SHAPED` or `W_SHAPED` there returns the 40/40/20 or 30/30/30/10 split above computed by HubSpot, not assembled by hand. One limit carries over whichever model is picked: this tool's numbers are scoped to the closed-won pipeline only — it has nothing to say about open opportunities. For the hybrid sales/marketing split, `query_crm_data` or `search_crm_objects` on DEAL records (any stage, filterable by source and owner) is the tool that answers "what percentage of sales-sourced deals had marketing touchpoints," not the attribution-report tool.

**Before promising any of this: the call itself can be scope-blocked, not just the spend fields below.** On a portal missing the `marketing.campaigns.read` scope, a real call to `get_campaign_attribution_reports` — asking for `REVENUE`/`DEAL_COUNT` by `ASSET_TYPE`, no different from the model calls described above — failed outright on that missing scope. The wording of the failure is not fixed: the same gap previously surfaced as the raw HubSpot API string `Insufficient scope: required "marketing.campaigns.read"`, and as of October 2026 surfaces as a connector message that names the scope and spells out the fix instead — reconnect the HubSpot app and explicitly check the permission (it is never granted by default, and this step needs no admin), and escalate to a HubSpot admin only if the permission shows a lock icon, meaning an admin has already disabled it for this app. That's the same scope gating the CAMPAIGN object's spend properties in Channel ROI Framework below, but here it blocks the attribution-model computation itself, for every one of the nine models, not just the spend rollup. Check this early with one real call before writing "attribution model: U-shaped" into a client-facing plan — and try the reconnect step before escalating to an admin, not the other way round.

**The silent under-count nobody budgets for.** A deal only enters this attribution pipeline if every one of five conditions holds: closed-won, has at least one associated contact, that contact was attached to the deal *before* the deal closed, amount/create date/close date are all populated, and the close date falls in the report window. The one that bites in practice: attaching the champion or economic buyer to the deal record *after* the contract is signed — the common cleanup pattern when sales adds contacts as the deal closes rather than as they're identified — does not retroactively create attribution. The deal simply doesn't show up under any channel, and a report that only looks at totals reads as "lower revenue this month," not "some deals fell out of the pipeline." One exception: a deal manually associated with a campaign object directly (not through contact influence) is always counted, regardless of timing or stage. When a channel's attributed revenue looks implausibly low against a known signed deal, check contact-association timing before concluding the channel underperformed.

### What No Model Tells You

Every attribution model has structural blindspots. Name them before presenting the results:
- **Dark social** — Conversations in Slack communities, private LinkedIn DMs, word-of-mouth referrals. Unmeasurable by any touchpoint model. Use CRM source surveys: "How did you first hear about us?" as a data supplement.
- **Offline touches** — Events, conference conversations, introductions through mutual connections. Track manually in CRM as offline touchpoints.
- **Multi-stakeholder journeys** — In enterprise deals, different stakeholders engage with different channels. Account-level attribution is more accurate than contact-level for B2B. The 6-to-10-stakeholder range often attributed to Gartner has kept drifting upward through 2026: Gartner's own more recent figure, as cited by secondary sources, is 11–20 for large enterprise deals (versus "five a decade ago"), and Forrester adds sharper cuts by deal size — roughly 13 stakeholders as a flat average (Forrester's *State of Business Buying, 2024* report), an 11.2 median specifically above $50k (Forrester + 6sense), and 14–23 for deals above $1M (Forrester's *B2B Buying Study*, 2023), with one 2026 citation of a 22-person "full decision network" for the most complex enterprise deals. None of these reports — Gartner's or Forrester's own site included — could be opened directly this session (same proxy block already logged against this domain family since 2026-08-30); what's here is a secondary-source summary of named analyst reports, not a number read off the report itself. Don't publish a single figure without checking it against what the client's own reps report seeing on calls. Mid-market deals ($25k–$100k ACV) still run smaller by every source checked, at 4 to 7.
- **Early-stage brand** — Companies with low brand awareness and short histories have too few deals to produce statistically reliable attribution data. Below 30 closed deals in the comparison window, directional is the honest framing — not precise. That number is a working floor, not a published one: no analyst source (Gartner, Forrester, Benchmarkit, ICONIQ) sets a minimum deal count for channel-comparison confidence in B2B. The nearest official reference point is Google's Data-Driven Attribution minimums — but the two platforms no longer share one number as of 2026. Google Ads dropped its eligibility floor (previously ~300 conversions + 3,000 interactions/30 days): DDA is now the default for every conversion action regardless of volume, and the old figure survives only as a performance recommendation (~200 conversions + 2,000 interactions/30 days), not a gate. GA4 did not follow — it still requires 400 conversions for the action and 20,000 for the property in the lookback window, and falls back to last-click silently, with no warning in the UI, below that. Either way this is a bidding-algorithm threshold, not a CRM dashboard one, so it does not transfer directly to the 30-deal floor above — cited only to show that Google's own platforms disagree on where "enough data" starts.
