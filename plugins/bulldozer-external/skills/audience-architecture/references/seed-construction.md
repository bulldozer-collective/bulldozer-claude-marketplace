# Closed-Won Seed Construction

**Seed construction protocol:**

**Step 1: Export from CRM**
- Filter: Deal Stage = Closed Won, Close Date = last 24 months
- Segment by deal size (separate seeds for SMB / mid-market / enterprise — mixing them dilutes precision)
- Minimum seed size, and it's platform-dependent — don't apply LinkedIn's floor to Google or vice versa:
  - LinkedIn Predictive Audiences require 300+ members in the source (a CRM list, a Matched Audience, or Lead Gen Form submitters) before it will build an expansion at all.
  - Google Customer Match now accepts lists from 100 users (lowered from the old 1,000-user floor across all networks, including Search) — a segment too thin for LinkedIn's 300 can still be worth uploading on Google alone. Don't wait for LinkedIn-scale volume before shipping the Google side. That 100-user floor (in force since May 2024) comes with a companion rule worth carrying into Step 6's refresh cadence: a list only stays eligible while at least 100 of its members were added or updated within the trailing ~540 days — and the same 540 days is also the hard cap on how long any single member counts at all, not just a minimum-activity window. A seed built once and never touched can quietly drop below the serving threshold well before anyone notices flat delivery and thinks to check the list itself. (Still not read on a live Google page — `support.google.com` stayed unreachable this pass too, now a flat DNS failure rather than the egress block logged since late August; two independent `WebSearch` passes naming the exact support pages, `answer/6379332` and `answer/6299717`, agree on both figures, which is the strongest confirmation this has had without a direct page read.)
- Include: company domain, contact email, first name, last name, country
- **Below the smaller platform's floor (100 on Google), don't fabricate volume by loosening the closed-won filter** (e.g. including Closed Lost or extending past 24 months) just to clear the number — a diluted seed produces a diluted expansion on both platforms. Wait, or combine adjacent deal-size segments from the segmentation table just below into one seed until there's enough real closed-won volume.

**Step 2: Clean before upload**
- Remove one-off enterprise deals that don't represent your typical buyer profile
- Remove deals that churned within 90 days (these represent a bad fit despite closed-won status)
- Remove competitors who bought to test your product
- Deduplicate by domain

**Step 3: Segment the seeds**
Build separate seeds per segment — each feeds a distinct expansion audience:

| Seed | Filter | Produces |
|------|--------|---------|
| Top LTV customers | Closed-won, top 20% by lifetime revenue | Highest-value account expansion |
| Fast-close deals | Closed-won, sales cycle < 45 days | Self-serve / high-intent buyer expansion |
| Best-fit industry | Closed-won, primary ICP industry only | Vertical-specific expansion |
| Expansion accounts | Customers who expanded MRR by 50%+ | Expansion-potential audience |

**Step 4: Upload and let the platform expand**
- LinkedIn: upload the seed as a Matched Audience, then build a Predictive Audience from it once the seed reports `READY` (a seed still `BUILDING` is refused outright). **Through Bulldozer OS, this second step is native too** — `bdzCreatePredictiveAudience` takes 1–20 seeds (any mix of our own matched-audience UUIDs on the same ad account, or a raw `dmpSegment`/`adForm`/`llaPartnerConversion` URN), a mandatory `locations` list (1–50 geo URNs — the only filter this tool supports, resolved via `bdzSearchAdTargetingEntities`), and a mandatory `desiredAudienceCount` — LinkedIn refuses the call without either. It comes back `BUILDING` with no usable id, same as any matched audience; poll `bdzGetAudience` until `READY`. There's no size dial beyond `desiredAudienceCount` — LinkedIn's model decides how far to expand from there. Per-account, there's also a sub-limit on how many Predictive Audiences you can hold at once. The clearest evidence yet points to 200: a fresh search indexing LinkedIn's own help page (`linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/94287`) returns that exact figure stated as an account limit, not just inferred from a page title about the Lookalike retirement — the older 30/100 figures (agency write-ups) now read as stale rather than as live disagreement. Still, no direct page read confirms any of the three numbers — `linkedin.com` and `support.google.com` both stayed unreachable this pass (a flat DNS failure, the same pattern logged since late September) — so treat "segment deliberately, don't spin one up per test" as the operating rule rather than banking on 200 either, and check the live count in Campaign Manager before planning a new wave. Since August 2026, LinkedIn also enforces an account-wide ceiling of 1,000 total DMP segments — Matched Audiences and Predictive Audiences combined, expired ones included; Bulldozer OS's own audience-creation tool confirms the same 1,000 figure directly (it surfaces the ceiling as an error rather than a published number to trust secondhand). A heavy user of both audience types can hit this larger, better-confirmed ceiling before the Predictive-Audience sub-limit ever becomes the binding constraint, whichever figure above turns out to be right.
- Google: upload the seed as a Customer Match list, then let Optimized Targeting / Audience Expansion run under Smart Bidding — this is a toggle on the campaign, not a percentage you set.
- Test by segment (Step 3's table), not by expansion size — the size lever from the old lookalike-% era doesn't exist on either platform anymore. **Even where a percentage still appears in the UI, don't treat it as a dial:** Google's Demand Gen Lookalike segments still label three reach tiers (Narrow/Balanced/Broad, roughly 2.5%/5%/10%), but since March 2026 Google's own AI can expand past the selected tier whenever it predicts better campaign performance — the label is a starting signal, not a hard cap. Compare cost per SQL between segments, not cost per lead.

**Building and refreshing the LinkedIn/Meta seed through Bulldozer OS, instead of a CSV each time:** `bdzCreateAudience` creates the empty matched audience directly on the ad account's platform — a LinkedIn DMP segment or a Meta customer-file audience. Google Customer Match isn't reachable this way; that upload still goes through the Google Ads account itself. Once created, `bdzAddAudienceMembers` pushes an explicit email/name list, or — when the seed is people who already filled a Bulldozer-tracked lead form rather than a fresh CRM export — `bdzAddAudienceMembersFromLeads` pulls them straight from the project's own captured leads, no export/re-import round trip. Two things catch a first attempt: on LinkedIn this needs the app's `rw_dmp_segments` permission, a separate product from the Advertising API — a token that already creates campaigns fine can still fail here — and the audience comes back `BUILDING` with no usable id for up to 48 hours on LinkedIn (poll `bdzGetAudience` until it reports `READY`), while Meta usually resolves within minutes. Before uploading, `bdzCountAudience` is worth a call on the intended targeting: LinkedIn returns a flat zero for anything under 300 matched members, meaning "too narrow to serve," not "empty" — a live confirmation of the 300-member floor above, not a number to take on faith. `bdzDeleteAudience` refuses to remove a segment a live campaign still targets, so drop it from the campaign's targeting first. This covers the seed only — the expansion step above it (building the actual Predictive Audience from that seed) is native too, through `bdzCreatePredictiveAudience` once the seed reports `READY`; see the LinkedIn bullet above for its required fields.
