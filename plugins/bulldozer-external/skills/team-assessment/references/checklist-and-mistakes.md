# Common Mistakes and Quality Checklist

## Common Mistakes

- **Scoring skills from self-report instead of direct observation.** A rep's own 1–5 rating of their discovery quality is not data — it's an opinion. Pull the score from call recordings, CRM notes, or a manager who has actually watched the rep work.
- **Running Step 6 before Steps 1–5.** An action plan written before the diagnosis is a wish list. Every P1/P2/P3 row must trace back to a specific finding from the 4-pillar audit, the quota attainment pattern, the skills matrix, the capacity model, or the alignment assessment — not to "things we already wanted to do."
- **Treating quota attainment as a people score.** A team where every rep is under 60% is a quota-setting or pipeline problem, not a hiring problem — see the pattern table in Step 2. Recommending replacements before checking the pattern is the single most common error in this kind of audit.
- **Skipping the cross-functional interviews.** The Marketing→SDR and SDR→AE diagnostics in Step 5 require actually asking people the two questions listed — not inferring the answer from dashboards. Handoff trust breaks in conversations, not in reports.
- **Recommending a hire as the first fix.** See the sequencing rule above. A capacity gap is real information, but it belongs at the end of the plan, after cheaper structural fixes have had a chance to close it.
- **Diagnosing a market problem as a team problem.** A team where attainment is low across every rep regardless of tenure or skill score is not automatically a coaching or hiring gap — see "When Not to Run the Full Audit." Producing a 30/60/90-day plan full of coaching and headcount asks for a team selling into a segment that doesn't convert just delays the real fix and burns the credibility of the next audit.
- **Applying a fixed coaching-timeline default (e.g. "30 days") regardless of sales cycle length.** A coaching window shorter than the team's own sales cycle can't produce a real result before the deadline — see the replacement threshold in Step 3. Set the clock to the cycle, not to a calendar convention.
- **Letting the action plan drift into generic advice.** "Improve communication between Sales and Marketing" is not an action plan row — it has no root cause, no owner, and no timeline. Every row must name the specific broken handoff or metric it fixes.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Every red flag cited in the 4-Pillar Audit traces to a specific metric or documented answer, not an impression
- [ ] Role clarity and skills gap scores come from direct observation, CRM data, or a structured interview — not self-report alone
- [ ] The quota attainment section names which pattern (Step 2 table) the team is in, not just the raw percentage
- [ ] The capacity model uses the team's actual historical attainment %, not the target attainment %
- [ ] All three cross-functional handoffs (Marketing→SDR, SDR→AE, AE→CS) were checked, even if only one turns out broken
- [ ] Every P1 and P2 row in the action plan has a named owner and a specific timeline — no blank cells left in the delivered version
- [ ] At least one process, data, or technology fix ranks above any hiring recommendation
- [ ] No item in the action plan restates a Step 1–5 finding without an action attached to it
- [ ] If attainment sits below the systemic threshold (Step 2) across the whole team, the plan names the ICP/market question as provisional-gating rather than proceeding straight to coaching or hiring rows
