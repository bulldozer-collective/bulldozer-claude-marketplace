# Budget Splits, Rebalancing Trigger and Stop Rule

## Step 4: Budget Allocation Framework — caveats and rules

**These splits are a starting heuristic, not a published benchmark.** No analyst report ties a percentage split to "demand creation / demand capture / demand conversion" by funding stage — that three-way split is practitioner GTM framing, not a category any analyst firm tracks. A handful of marketing-stats aggregator sites do publish numbers under this exact label, but none discloses a methodology or a sample, and they don't agree with each other — treat them the same as no source at all. Use the ranges above to open the conversation with the client's own numbers, not as a target to defend.

**Portfolio rebalancing trigger:** Measure each motion's cost per SQL, not cost per lead. If demand creation produces €350/SQL and demand capture produces €900/SQL in the same quarter, shift budget toward creation. Pull the actual spend and cost-per-conversion from each connected ad account (see the platform skills — Google Ads, Meta, LinkedIn — or paid-strategy for a cross-platform read) rather than asking the client to estimate it from memory. Rebalance quarterly based on 90-day data — 30-day data is too short for cross-channel attribution.

**Don't rebalance on a handful of SQLs, and don't rebalance before the sales cycle has had time to play out.** The same volume floor that governs channel comparisons in Step 6 applies to motions: a gap between two motions' cost per SQL means nothing below a few dozen SQLs per motion in the window, and a 90-day window is itself a starting point — if the real sales cycle runs longer, judging a motion at 90 days is judging the wrong indicator, the same trap Step 6 flags for channel-level numbers.

**Stop rule, not just a rebalancing rule.** A rebalancing trigger without a stop rule shifts budget around forever without ever cutting a motion loose. If a motion still misses its rebalancing trigger two quarters after being reallocated toward, pause it rather than let it run at a reduced but permanent budget — a motion that never clears the bar isn't underfunded, it's not working for this business.
