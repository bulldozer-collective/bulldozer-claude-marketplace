# Cross-Channel Measurement — Attribution, Data Quality and Monthly Checklist

## Step 6: Cross-Channel Measurement Framework — detail

**One source of truth per number, one attribution model per quarter.** Revenue and deal counts come from the CRM, spend from each platform, traffic from the analytics tool — never summed across systems, never blended into a "total marketing impact" figure. If the CRM holds deals in more than one currency, sum the home-currency-converted amount, not the raw deal amount — otherwise the "Pipeline from X" totals above silently mix currencies without converting them. And no attribution model recovers a truth that exists: linear, U-shaped, time-decay, first-touch, last-touch are credit-splitting conventions, not measurements. Pick one for the quarter, name it next to every number, and never switch it mid-quarter to flatter a channel.

**Confirm the attribution report itself is reachable before naming a model in a client deliverable — it can fail as a whole, not just its spend fields.** On a HubSpot-connected portal, the tool behind these named models (REVENUE/DEAL_AMOUNT by whichever model, DEAL_COUNT, CONTACT_COUNT) can be gated by the same missing scope discussed below for campaign spend — `marketing.campaigns.read` — even in a session where deal and revenue data on the CRM object itself reads normally. The error names the scope and what to do about it directly: "This tool needs permissions that the HubSpot connection doesn't have: marketing.campaigns.read. To grant them, reconnect the HubSpot app to the agent and select these permissions during reconnection..." — and warns separately that a permission showing a lock icon has been disabled by a HubSpot admin, in which case reconnecting won't help until they approve it for the app. A blocked call returns nothing, and nothing can pass for "no multi-touch pipeline this quarter" instead of "this report is switched off for this portal." Make one real call before writing an attribution model's name into a report; if it fails on this scope, act on exactly what the error says — have the connection reconnected with that permission checked, or escalate to a HubSpot admin if it shows as locked — rather than presenting HubSpot attribution modeling as available by default.

**Two more failure modes sit ahead of that scope gate, and neither one is a permissions problem.** The first call to this report in a session has to have read the tool's own usage instructions first — skip that and it comes back asking for that step instead of returning a report, which is a required retry, not an error to route to an admin. Separately, a query spanning a wide date range or many campaigns can come back marked "still processing" with a job to poll instead of the report itself — read that as "not ready yet," not as "blocked" or "no data." Whoever pulls this report for a client deliverable should expect both of these once, distinct from the scope failure above, before trusting an empty-looking result.

**Show the missing-data rate next to the number, not below it.** If 30% of contacts have no recorded first-touch source, every attribution figure in this report is built on the other 70% — that share belongs on the same line as the number, not in a footnote.

**A "Pipeline from X" number can silently undercount deals for a reason that has nothing to do with the channel.** In a CRM-native attribution report (e.g. HubSpot's campaign attribution), a deal only counts toward a channel if the contact was associated with the deal *before* the deal closed — a contact attached after the fact, which happens routinely on a late handoff from SDR to AE or a manual CRM cleanup, does not retroactively create attribution. A channel whose "Pipeline (90-day)" number looks weak may just be feeding deals through a process where the CRM linkage happens late, not a channel that's actually underperforming. Check the handoff timing before cutting a channel's budget on this number alone.

**"Multi-touch influenced pipeline" and a per-channel "Pipeline from X" number are not built the same way, and summing the wrong one across channels invents a bigger total than the real one.** A CRM-native attribution report documents this explicitly: a revenue figure weighted by the named attribution model sums to the true closed-won total, but the full, undivided deal value used for "influenced pipeline" reappears under every channel a deal touched — a single €50k deal touched by three channels shows €50k under each. That's the correct behavior for reading one channel's influence in isolation; it stops being correct the moment someone adds the per-channel figures together and compares the sum to total closed-won revenue. Read "Multi-touch influenced pipeline" one channel at a time, never as a total.

**The window in the table above ("90-day") doesn't always filter the same date field.** In the same attribution report, a revenue or deal-value figure filters on the deal's close date, a deal-count figure filters on the deal's create date, and a contact- or touchpoint-count figure filters on the date of the touchpoint itself. Two rows that both say "90-day" can silently describe two different 90-day populations — confirm which date field a metric filters on before reading two rows of the table side by side as the same period.

**Spend for a cost-per-SQL or ROAS calculation rarely lives next to the revenue number, and it isn't always reachable at all.** Deal and revenue data comes from the CRM's attribution report; spend and budget are typically a separate, manually-maintained figure on the campaign record itself (in HubSpot, campaign-level spend/budget roll-up fields, not the attribution report). Two failure modes look the same from the outside but need different fixes: if the field is empty because no one entered spend line items, say the ROAS can't be computed, not compute one against a zero or missing denominator. If the connector can't read the campaign object at all, the error you get back isn't always the same, and only one form tells you what to actually do: sometimes it reads like a generic expired session or expired token, and sometimes it names the missing permission directly and says how to fix it — on HubSpot's connector, the current wording is "This tool needs permissions that the HubSpot connection doesn't have: marketing.campaigns.read. To grant them, reconnect the HubSpot app to the agent and select these permissions during reconnection — they are not granted by default and must be explicitly checked," with a separate warning that a permission showing a lock icon has been disabled by a HubSpot admin and won't be fixed by reconnecting. Either way, check whether it's actually a missing permission grant on that specific object before telling anyone to retry later — a CRM connector can leave deal data fully readable (the DEAL object stays available) while gating the campaign object behind a permission an admin has to switch on separately. When the error names the permission, act on it directly — reconnect with it checked, or ask the HubSpot admin to approve it for the app if it shows as locked — rather than requesting a generic "Campaigns access" grant. Read the error as a permission question first, a session question second.

**Don't compare below volume, and respect the cycle.** Below a few dozen conversions in the window, a cost-per-SQL gap between channels is noise, not signal. And the 90-day windows above are a starting point, not a rule: measure at the length of the real sales cycle, or every early read is wrong by design.

**Cross-channel measurement checklist (run monthly):**
- [ ] Every number has one source system named next to it, never a sum across systems
- [ ] The attribution model is stated, and hasn't changed since last month without a documented reason
- [ ] The attribution report call itself was tested this session and returned data, not assumed available — a scope gap can block the whole report, not just campaign spend fields, and a "still processing" or "read the instructions first" response isn't a failure either
- [ ] The % of contacts with a usable source is shown next to the channel breakdown
- [ ] Revenue totals use the home-currency-converted deal amount when the CRM holds deals in more than one currency, not the raw amount — otherwise the sum silently mixes currencies
- [ ] No comparison is drawn from a volume below what Step 1's alignment session agreed is meaningful
- [ ] Each motion's cost-per-SQL is recalculated and checked against the Step 4 rebalancing trigger
- [ ] A weak "Pipeline from X" number has been checked against handoff/CRM-linkage timing before it's read as a channel problem
- [ ] Spend used in any cost-per-SQL or ROAS figure is confirmed present and reachable (not zero, missing, or blocked by a connector permission on the campaign object) before the ratio is reported
- [ ] "Multi-touch influenced pipeline" is read per channel, never summed across channels and compared to total revenue
- [ ] Any two rows compared side by side filter on the same date field (close date, create date, or touchpoint date) — not just the same window length
