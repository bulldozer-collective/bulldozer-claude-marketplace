# LTV:CAC Ratio Origin and CAC Ceiling Notes

**Where this ratio comes from, and its limits:** the "3x" reference traces back to venture guidance popularized in early SaaS-metrics writing over a decade ago, not a live external benchmark that gets re-measured yearly. Surveys that report a current median disagree with each other and move with segment (enterprise vs. mid-market vs. SMB ACV) — a repeat check of 2026-dated vendor content still finds the same 3–5x band and the same segment split (one source citing SMB around 2.5x against enterprise around 4.5x, blended median around 3.2x), plus growth stage as a further axis (early-stage companies converge lower, scale-stage higher) — no single figure holds across every company shape, and none of this comes from an audited analyst report. A larger-sample 2026 survey (Optifai, self-reported at 939 B2B SaaS companies) reports the identical three-way split (SMB ~2.5x, mid-market ~3.2x, enterprise ~4.5x) — one more point of convergence, still not an audited analyst source. Use the bands above to flag a ratio that's clearly broken (below 2x) or clearly loose (above 5x), and lean on this company's own trend across cohort vintages for anything in between.

**This ceiling is conservative by construction whenever there's real expansion.** It's built off MRR at acquisition (M0) — if the revenue cohort table (Step 2) shows NRR consistently above 100% for a channel, the true payback window is shorter than M0 MRR alone implies, because gross profit compounds as those accounts expand. Raising the ceiling to reflect expected expansion is a legitimate call once two or three cohort vintages confirm the expansion pattern holds for that specific channel or segment — never off the first cohort, and never applied uniformly across channels that haven't independently shown the same expansion behavior.
