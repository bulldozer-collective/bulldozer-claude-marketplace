# Trend Interpretation and Allocation Table

**Positive trends (growing quality):**
- Newer cohorts retain at higher rates through M6 → onboarding improvements are working
- CAC payback shortening → acquisition efficiency improving or expansion accelerating
- NRR increasing quarter over quarter → customer success and expansion motions strengthening

**Negative trends (degrading quality):**
- Newer cohorts churn faster → ICP drift (acquiring wrong customers), product regression, or a step-change in onboarding quality that hasn't been caught yet
- CAC payback lengthening → rising acquisition costs, a channel running into diminishing returns, or gross margin compression eating into the same MRR
- GRR falling while NRR holds steady → expansion revenue from existing accounts is masking real churn; this is fragile and reverses fast if expansion slows
- Payback improving in the blended view but flat or worse channel by channel → the improvement is a mix-shift toward a cheaper channel, not a real efficiency gain — don't credit the team for it

**Before acting on any trend, check the sample size.** A cohort of 8 customers moving from 90% to 75% retention is one customer churning, not a trend. Read a cohort's month-over-month change as signal only once it holds across at least two consecutive vintages of comparable size — one data point is an anecdote.

**Allocation recommendation table:**

| Channel/Segment | Payback vs. ceiling | LTV:CAC trend | Retention vs. blended | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outbound | Under ceiling | Improving | Above blended | Scale spend |
| Content | At ceiling | Flat | At blended | Hold, investigate onboarding for this channel's cohorts |
| Paid | Over ceiling | Declining | Below blended | Cut spend, re-examine targeting before any reinvestment |

**What this analysis cannot tell you on its own:** it shows where dollars are working today, not where the next dollar should go if you're entering a new segment or channel with no cohort history yet. Treat a channel with fewer than a handful of cohort vintages as directional, not decisive — say so explicitly in the recommendation rather than presenting it with the same confidence as a channel with two years of data behind it.
