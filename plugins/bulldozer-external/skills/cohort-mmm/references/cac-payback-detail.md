# CAC Payback: Spend Definition, Worked Example and Benchmarks

**What "spend" means is the single biggest lever in this calculation, and it's usually left implicit.** Ad spend alone systematically understates CAC — it leaves out SDR and AE salaries and commission, marketing headcount, the tools stack (CRM, marketing automation, attribution), and the first 90 days of onboarding cost. Non-analyst SaaS-finance sources agree on the direction (media-only always understates) but not on the size of the gap, and a repeat check keeps widening that disagreement rather than narrowing it: alongside the multiples already found (a payroll/benefits overhead factor around 1.3x on headcount cost, a paid-vs-blended-channel gap around 2.4–3.1x), one more source gives a single named before/after example — $45 media-only versus $78 fully loaded, a 73% increase, so roughly 1.7x — for one unnamed company. None of these are audited, none share a methodology, and the range they span (1.3x to over 3x) means "several times higher" is itself an overstatement as often as an understatement. Treat the direction as certain and the multiple as unknowable from public sources — recompute it from this company's own headcount and tooling cost rather than borrowing any of these figures. What's not optional: state explicitly, every time a payback number is reported, whether it's built on media spend alone or on fully-loaded S&M cost. A payback of "4 months" on media spend and a payback of "14 months" fully loaded aren't two different findings — they're the same channel described two different ways, and reporting only the first without saying so is misleading by omission.

**CAC payback benchmarks:**

| ACV | Acceptable | Healthy | Watch Signal |
|-----|-----------|---------|-------------|
| <€5K | 6–10 months | <6 months | >12 months |
| €5K–€25K | 10–15 months | <12 months | >18 months |
| €25K–€100K | 14–20 months | <18 months | >24 months |
| €100K+ | 18–30 months | <24 months | >36 months |

**Treat this table as a starting heuristic, not a validated external norm.** No analyst source (Gartner, Forrester, ICONIQ) publishes a payback-by-ACV table; what circulates under this shape comes from vendor surveys and blogs whose own median payback figures disagree by several months for the same year — still true on a repeat check: 2026-dated vendor content quotes anywhere from a ~15–18-month blended median (one source citing a rise from 14 to 18 months since 2023, another naming that same rise as +29%) down to sub-6-month top-quartile figures, and several now split the same ACV band further by growth stage (early-stage vs. scale-stage) or GTM motion, which only widens the spread further — one such split even shows payback getting *longer*, not shorter, as a company matures past early stage, the opposite of what intuition suggests. The same repeat check surfaces yet another ACV split (an 11-month median under €5K ACV, 22 months at €50K–€100K) that lands inside this table's own bands but was produced independently, by a different vendor, with no shared methodology — agreement between two unaudited sources is not confirmation, it's coincidence until one of them shows its work. None of this reconciles into one citable number; it reinforces the opposite lesson, that the external band is for a sanity check only. A repeat check turns up one source with a clearer methodology than the rest — a named 2026 vendor survey (Aleph × Benchmarkit, 342 B2B SaaS/AI-native companies, 198 of which reported this specific metric for full-year 2025) puts the blended median at 16 months, down from 18 the year before — an 11% year-over-year improvement, described as the largest single-year move in four years of that dataset — with segment bands of roughly 8–12 months (SMB, sub-$15K ACV), 14–18 (mid-market), and 18–24 (enterprise, $100K+ ACV). Those bands sit inside this table's own numbers, but the direction is the opposite of another source already cited above (a multi-year rise from 14 to 18 months): two named vendor surveys now disagree not just on the size of the median but on which way it's moving. Still a vendor-run survey, not an audited analyst report — read only through search synthesis, since a direct fetch of the report page failed (access blocked in this environment, consistent with every prior attempt since end of August). A third, differently-sourced 2026 survey (Optifai, self-reported at 939 B2B SaaS companies — a larger claimed sample than either Aleph or the source above) lands on the same order of magnitude by segment — an 11-month median under $5K ACV, 22 months at $50K–$100K — again read only through search synthesis, no primary source opened directly. Use any of this to sanity-check an order of magnitude, then recalibrate against this company's own trailing cohorts — a channel's own payback trend (below) is always the more trustworthy signal than where it sits against an external band.

**Annual prepay breaks the payback math if you don't say which clock you're on.** The formula above tracks recognized MRR emerging month by month — but a customer who prepays a year upfront hands over the cash on day one, so the business recovers its CAC in cash terms immediately even though the recognized-revenue version of the same table shows payback stretching out over the full 12 months. Bulldozer OS can tell the two apart when the client bills through HubSpot's own Commerce objects: the Subscription object's `hs_recurring_billing_frequency` (monthly, quarterly, annually, and further multi-year steps) and Line Items' `hs_recurring_billing_period` / `hs_tcv` carry the real billing cadence per customer, separately from the deal amount. Before running this step, check whether the cohort mixes billing frequencies; if it does, decide once whether the question being answered is "when do we recover the cash" (bill-date basis) or "when does the P&L show it recovered" (MRR-recognition basis), and label the output with whichever one it is. Silently blending monthly and annual customers into one payback trajectory makes annual-heavy cohorts look artificially slow.
