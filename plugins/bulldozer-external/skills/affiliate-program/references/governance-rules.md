# Governance Rules

**Write these before the first partner signs, not after the first dispute.** A program without written rules resolves every disagreement ad hoc, which partners read as unfair even when the outcome itself is reasonable.

**Brand bidding.** Default to prohibiting affiliates from bidding on the brand name, close misspellings, and product names in paid search — list the exact protected terms in the partner agreement rather than a vague "don't bid on our brand." An affiliate who out-bids the brand's own paid-search campaigns is paid twice for the same customer: a higher CPC on the brand's own ads, and a commission on a conversion that would likely have happened anyway. If partners are allowed to run paid search at all, restrict it to a named, approved subset and audit their campaigns before approving them.

**Self-referral and channel conflict.** Affiliates cannot refer themselves, family members, or their own employer. For B2B specifically, also rule on the harder case in writing: what happens when a lead would have converted through organic search or direct sales outreach, but an affiliate's link touched the deal first. Position-based attribution (Step 2) reduces this dispute but doesn't eliminate it — give sales a documented escalation path for "this affiliate credit looks wrong" rather than a rule that pretends the conflict never happens.

**Coupon and cashback browser extensions are a named version of this problem, not a hypothetical one.** Extensions like Honey and Capital One Shopping have faced active litigation since early 2025 over exactly this mechanism — a hidden tab redirecting the browser to insert the extension's own affiliate ID right before checkout, overwriting whichever affiliate actually drove the sale. The case against PayPal's Honey extension is still live, not settled or dismissed: a motion to dismiss was denied on June 22, 2026 and the case has since moved into discovery, with no trial date before late 2027 at the earliest. Three major affiliate networks sanctioned Honey over this exact behavior within the same two-week window in January 2026 — Rakuten Advertising terminated it outright on January 12 (cutting roughly 2,000 merchants, including Walmart and Sephora), Impact.com suspended it on January 16–17, and Awin confirmed the policy breach and froze Honey's payments and access to new advertiser programs on January 21 — independent networks, independent investigations, the same conclusion. But network exclusion turned out to be a reversible sanction, not a lasting one: Rakuten reinstated Honey on May 6, 2026, after Honey adopted a stand-down SDK Rakuten open-sourced for exactly this purpose (it lets an extension detect that a click already carries another affiliate's referral and yield automatically, rather than overwriting it). The reinstatement drew public criticism for coming without a financial penalty matched to the scale of the alleged violations, and it's not known whether Impact.com or Awin followed Rakuten back to reinstatement — treat each network's current stance as something to check at the time, not something this skill can freeze in place. The lesson for a governance section isn't "an affiliate network will police this for you" — a network can drop a partner and readmit it a few months later once a checkbox is satisfied. The documented cases are e-commerce and creator programs, not B2B SaaS specifically — but the mechanism (an extension firing a click right before checkout) doesn't care what's being sold. If coupon or cashback partners are allowed in the program at all, exclude them from last-click override and require the same deal registration as any other affiliate, and don't treat "this network doesn't currently list Honey" as a durable proof of anything — don't let a browser extension outrank a partner who spent months in the sales cycle.

**Deal registration disputes.** When two partners register the same company, the first-registered affiliate holds the deal (Step 2). Write this down before it happens — the alternative is a judgment call that looks arbitrary to whoever loses it.

**AE commission on partner-sourced deals.** Decide, in writing, whether the account executive earns their normal self-sourced commission rate on a deal that arrives already registered by a partner who is also being paid on it, or a reduced, assisted-deal rate — the same logic most comp plans already apply to marketing-sourced or inbound leads versus deals the rep prospected themselves. Paying both the partner's full commission and the AE's full self-sourced rate on the same deal isn't generous, it's an unplanned CAC increase on every partner-sourced dollar of revenue. Decide the split before the first partner-sourced deal closes — not while negotiating a plan exception with a rep after the fact.

**Termination and clawback enforcement.** Reserve the right to remove a partner for policy violations, not only for inactivity. Name what triggers removal — cookie stuffing, misleading product claims, spam — and state what happens to pending commissions when a partner is removed for cause versus leaving voluntarily.

**Payment terms.** State the payout schedule, the minimum payout threshold, and the payment method, and tie the first payout date to the close of the clawback window (Step 1) rather than to the conversion date. Ambiguity on payment terms is one of the most common sources of partner complaints in any referral or affiliate program — say it once, in writing, before it's asked.

**Collect tax forms before the first payment, not after.** A US-based affiliate needs a W-9 on file; a non-US affiliate needs a W-8BEN (or W-8BEN-E for an entity) — the payer is required to hold this before paying, and skipping it triggers backup withholding (24%) or treaty withholding (up to 30%) on the payment instead of a clean payout. For 2026, the US 1099-NEC reporting threshold rose from $600 to $2,000 per payee per year (signed into law July 2025, effective for payments made from January 1, 2026, indexed to inflation from 2027) — fewer low-volume affiliates will trigger a form than before, but the threshold doesn't change when the tax form has to already be on file: collect it at onboarding, regardless of how small the first payout looks.
