# Tracking Platform Requirements and Options

**Required capabilities for B2B SaaS:**
- Recurring-revenue commission engine (calculates monthly commission based on customer billing events, not one-time conversions)
- Long attribution windows: configurable from 30 to 365+ days per deal type
- Subscription-event integration with billing platform (Stripe, Chargebee, Recurly) — commission triggers on payment, not on form fill
- Manual deal registration: affiliate can submit company + contact name for enterprise deals outside click-window
- Partner portal: affiliate sees their links, clicks, conversions, and payout status without contacting your team
- Fraud detection: identify self-referrals, suspicious traffic patterns, cookie stuffing
- CRM integration: affiliate deal registration syncs to HubSpot/Salesforce so sales team sees referral source. **On HubSpot specifically, this is not a native field** — there is no standard `deal_source` property, and the built-in "Original Traffic Source" property only distinguishes generic channels (organic, paid, direct, referral…), not named partners. Create a dedicated custom deal property (e.g. a partner picklist) and confirm the tracking platform's HubSpot integration writes to it, rather than assuming a partner name shows up on the deal by default. **Don't reach for HubSpot's native `PARTNER_CLIENT` object as a shortcut here** — despite the name, it models the relationship between a HubSpot Solutions Partner agency and that agency's own HubSpot-customer accounts, not a relationship between your portal and your own affiliates or resellers. On a standard portal it isn't even readable, let alone writable: the object only becomes visible through the API once the portal is itself configured as an active HubSpot Solutions Partner account. And where it is visible, its commission fields (`hs_projected_commission`, `hs_managed_mrr`…) describe what HubSpot pays the agency for managing that client's account — a different commission from the one your own affiliate program pays its own affiliates, never a field to repurpose for it. The custom deal property above is still the only real path.

**Platform options by stage (illustrative — check each vendor's current feature set against the list above before committing, platform positioning shifts year to year, and this tier of the market has been consolidating fast — verify who currently owns a given name before treating a two-year-old comparison article as still accurate):**
- Pre-product-market-fit / first 10 partners: a Stripe-native, developer-friendly tool built to sit on top of existing billing (Rewardful is the reference example) — quick to set up, sufficient for simple recurring commission.
- 10–100 partners (growth stage): a B2B-SaaS-native partner platform with a built-in partner marketplace and CRM sync (PartnerStack is the category leader here) or a broader enterprise-attribution tool (Impact.com) — multi-currency, more partner type configuration, stronger analytics. **PartnerStack itself changed hands in April 2026** — AppDirect acquired it to build what AppDirect calls a "unified subscription commerce platform for partner-led growth." PartnerStack's own post-acquisition announcement says there are no changes to the current PartnerStack experience — same programs, same points of contact — but that is the acquirer's own framing right after close, not a lasting guarantee: confirm at purchase time whether it's still sold as a standalone PRM tool or now bundled with AppDirect's marketplace infrastructure, rather than assuming the pricing and packaging a comparison article describes from before April 2026 still holds.
- 100+ partners / complex program: enterprise partner-marketing platforms with deep multi-tier attribution and CRM sync (Impact.com, Partnerize, TUNE are the established names). TUNE has been under Constellation Software's Perseus group since 2020, now run alongside CAKE under one unified leadership team — still an active, independent-feeling option. Ownership at the top of this tier is moving fast enough that a name alone isn't proof of independence: confirm current ownership directly with the vendor rather than trusting last year's category map.

**Don't pick by category label.** A tool marketed as a generic "affiliate plugin" (built first for e-commerce checkouts) and a tool marketed as B2B-SaaS-native both change their feature sets over time — some general-purpose plugins have added recurring-commission and CRM-registration support since being written off as e-commerce-only. Score any candidate against the required-capabilities list above directly, rather than trusting whether its marketing calls it "SaaS" or "e-commerce."

**Don't rely on third-party cookies as the primary tracking mechanism.** Google confirmed in April 2025 it would keep third-party cookies available by default in Chrome rather than force a phaseout, and retired the remaining Privacy Sandbox APIs in October 2025 over low industry adoption — but that reversal doesn't make cookie-based tracking dependable. Safari's Intelligent Tracking Prevention and Firefox's Enhanced Tracking Protection already strip third-party cookies by default, and ad blockers do the rest, independently of whatever Chrome decides. Require a first-party or server-side tracking path — a server-to-server postback, or a conversion API call fired on your own billing event — as a checklist item when scoring a platform, not a nice-to-have. A program that can only prove attribution through a cookie a browser vendor can strip at any time is one policy change away from an unexplained drop in tracked conversions.
