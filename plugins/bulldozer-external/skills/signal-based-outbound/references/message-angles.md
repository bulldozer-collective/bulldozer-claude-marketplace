# Message Angle Per Signal Type

## Message Angle Per Signal Type — templates

### Funding announcement
**Window:** Act within 3–7 days of announcement  
**Angle:** Budget to deploy, headcount to hire, systems to build  
**Opening frame:** "Companies at your stage typically use the 90 days post-raise to [build/fix/scale X]. We've worked with [3 companies at similar stage] through that exact transition."

### New executive hire (VP+)
**Window:** Act 14–21 days post-start date (not day 1 — too early signals surveillance)  
**Angle:** New leader evaluates vendors in the first 90 days. Position as a peer resource, not a sales call  
**Opening frame:** "New [role] typically spend their first quarter auditing [function] and making a few decisive bets. We've helped [peer company] get [specific outcome] — curious what's top of the list for you."

### Tech stack change (competitor dropped)
**Window:** Act within 7–10 days of detection  
**Angle:** Gap in the stack creates procurement urgency  
**Opening frame:** "Noticed [Company] recently moved off [Competitor]. A lot of teams we work with made that same switch and then needed to [fill capability gap]. We help with that specific transition."

### Job posting match
**Window:** Act within 7–14 days of posting  
**Angle:** The job posting reveals the problem — reflect it back  
**Opening frame:** "Saw you're hiring a [Role] — the JD mentions [specific requirement from posting]. That's usually a signal that [underlying problem] has become a priority. We've built that for a few companies at your stage."

### G2 comparison / competitor mention
**Window:** Act within 24–48 hours — this is the highest-precision, shortest-decay signal  
**Angle:** They're actively evaluating. Position your differentiation, not your feature list  
**Opening frame:** "Saw [Company] is researching [category/competitor]. We often talk to teams at this stage in the evaluation — happy to share how we compare on [the dimension that matters most for their use case]."

### First-party intent (pricing page / demo page)
**Window:** Same day. Within 4 hours is the standard for Tier 1  
**Angle:** They came to you. Don't pretend you don't know  
**Opening frame:** "Saw [Company] has been spending time on our [pricing/demo] page — figured it made sense to reach out directly rather than wait. [One sentence on their likely situation based on their vertical/stage.] Happy to answer any questions directly."

**This signal has a compliance surface, not just a "does it feel creepy" one — check before naming the exact page.** U.S. website-tracking litigation (California's wiretapping statute, CIPA, applied to session tracking and identification tools) is still high-volume and genuinely unsettled, not trending cleanly either way. The appeal most likely to set the first real precedent on whether CIPA reaches website-tracking technology at all — *Variety Media, LLC v. Superior Court* (Cal. Ct. App.) — had oral argument in late August 2026 and a tentative ruling finding the pen-register statute *can* reach internet communications in general, while the specific claim in front of it (built on IP address alone) still failed as pleaded; no final published opinion yet as of this writing. Below that appeal, trial courts remain openly split — several conflicting rulings landed within the same ten-day stretch in 2026 — and defendants picked up a real win this year with class-certification denied in at least one long-running tracking case, which several law firms flagged as significant. Visitor-identification vendors (the same category as Warmly, RB2B, HubSpot Breeze Intelligence cited above) sit squarely inside what's being challenged either way. The legislative fix that could have changed the exposure broadly (California SB 690, which would have exempted ordinary business tracking from CIPA) didn't pass in that broad form — what's now law only moves enforcement of one narrow provision (pen register/trap-and-trace, Section 638.51) from private lawsuits to the Attorney General alone, starting January 1, 2027, and leaves the wiretapping/session-replay theory that visitor-ID tools fall under untouched. Don't treat any specific case count, settlement figure, or "tide turning" framing as durable — this keeps moving in both directions — but as of this writing there is no legislative or appellate relief yet for the specific risk this signal carries: run the visitor-ID tool and the exact wording of "we saw you on our pricing page" past whoever owns legal/compliance risk before shipping it at volume, the same way a domain change goes through deliverability review before a send. Where that review hasn't happened, open on the *company-level* interest ("been thinking about [category]") rather than the individual's tracked page visit.
