# Community Health Metrics — why these aren't benchmarks, and what to do when they stay red

The four targets in the main skill file (DAU/MAU >15%, new member post rate >25%, thread reply
rate >60%, staff % of posts <30%) are directional heuristics, not numbers to defend in a QBR.
Read this before presenting them to a client as if they were an industry standard.

## No published number is specific to community engagement

None of the four targets is a published market benchmark for community engagement
specifically. Retention and engagement thresholds don't transfer across business models: a 15%
DAU/MAU floor that's healthy for a daily-workflow tool is meaningless for a community that's
naturally weekly (e.g. a practitioner group that meets around a Tuesday ritual thread).
Establish the community's own baseline in week 3–4 of the launch, then track movement against
*that* — a rising or falling trend against your own history is the signal, a static external
percentage is not.

**When a real, named benchmark does turn up, resist borrowing it anyway if it's measuring a
different behavior.** Mixpanel's 2026 State of Digital Analytics report (12,000+ companies)
puts overall B2B SaaS product stickiness around 25–35% DAU/WAU — a real, dated, officially
published number, unlike most of what circulates for community engagement. It still doesn't
answer "is this community healthy," because it measures logging into the product, not
participating in a community: a member can be a daily product user and a total community
lurker, or the reverse. A product-stickiness benchmark and a community-participation benchmark
are not interchangeable just because both produce a DAU/MAU-shaped percentage.

The most promising named candidate found to date, Higher Logic's *2025 Association Community
Benchmark Report*, doesn't fit either: it measures absolute monthly counts (563 unique logins,
1,156 total logins, 68 contributors, 163 discussion actions/month on average), not percentages
comparable to the targets above, and its population is professional-association/non-profit
communities — not B2B SaaS product communities. Don't re-chase this specific report in future
passes; if a source is worth retrying, look for one measuring B2B *product* communities
specifically, in percentages, not absolute counts.

## Staff % of posts only means something after the open launch

"Staff % of posts <30%" only means something after the open launch. During Weeks 1–2 of the
launch playbook, staff *should* be writing most first posts and every reply — that's the job
description of seeding a room, not a red flag. Reading this metric against a fixed threshold
before Week 4 will tell you to panic about a room that's working exactly as planned. Start
tracking it from the open launch onward, not from day one.

## What to do when the warning signs are still true 6–8 weeks after open launch

Don't keep running the same rituals hoping the next week is different — that's optimizing a
channel, not fixing a community. Two moves, and only two: fold the community into a
lower-effort format it can actually sustain (a monthly newsletter roundup of the best threads, a
quarterly call — something one person can run without a platform to moderate), or pause
new-member invites entirely and spend a month on the 5–10 people who *do* show up before
inviting anyone else. Continuing to invite new members into a room where questions go
unanswered for days makes the warning signs worse, not better — it adds more people to
disappoint, not more energy to fix it.
