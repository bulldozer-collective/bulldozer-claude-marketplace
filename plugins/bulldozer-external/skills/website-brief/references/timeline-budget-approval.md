# Section 9 Timeline and Budget and Section 10 Approval Process

**A date without a milestone breakdown is a wish, not a plan.** Work backward from go-live and name what happens in each phase — this is what lets the agency tell you in week 1 whether the date is realistic.

**Format:**

```
Target go-live: [date]

| Phase | Milestone | Target date | Owner |
|-------|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Discovery | Brief finalized, kickoff call | [date] | [Client / Agency] |
| Content | All P1 copy delivered and approved | [date] | [Client] |
| Design | Wireframes approved | [date] | [Client] |
| Design | Visual design approved | [date] | [Client] |
| Development | Staging site ready for review | [date] | [Agency] |
| QA | Content, links, tracking, forms tested | [date] | [Agency] |
| Launch | DNS cutover, go-live | [date] | [Agency + Client] |

Budget: [total, or range] — [fixed scope / time and materials]
Budget breakdown (if fixed scope): [design: X / development: X / copywriting: X / photography or video: X / contingency: X]
```

**What actually slips timelines:** content delivery and approval rounds — not design or development. If Section 7 shows the client owns copy and no delivery dates are set, the go-live date in this section is not credible yet. Fix Section 7 before finalizing this one.

**Contingency:** Reserve 10–15% of budget and 1–2 weeks of schedule for scope discovered during discovery (a page nobody accounted for, a CMS migration more involved than expected). Note this reserve explicitly rather than absorbing it silently into P1 scope.

**Name who approves what, at which stage, before work starts.** The single most common cause of blown timelines is a client stakeholder rejecting work at the final review that an earlier approver had already signed off on.

**Required fields:**
- **Approval stages:** [e.g., wireframes → visual design → staging/QA → go-live] — each stage needs one approver, not a group
- **Final approver:** [name and title] — the person whose "yes" ends the review round. If two people can say no independently, name which one's "no" wins.
- **Review turnaround:** [business days the client commits to for feedback at each stage] — an open-ended review window is where timelines die
- **Revision rounds included:** [e.g., 2 rounds of feedback per stage in scope; a 3rd round is a change order]
- **Feedback format:** [consolidated written comments in one document/tool, not a stream of individual Slack messages or a call with no notes]

**Template:**
```
Approval stages: [list in order]
Final approver: [Name, Title] — [email]
Secondary reviewers (input, not veto): [Name/Title — what they review for]
Review turnaround: [X business days per stage]
Revisions included: [X rounds per stage]
Feedback format: [tool/document]
```

**Rule:** If the final approver has not seen and does not need to see every intermediate stage (e.g., a CEO who only reviews the homepage and pricing page), say so explicitly. Silently assuming who needs to sign off on what is how a CEO ends up rejecting an approved design in week 6.
